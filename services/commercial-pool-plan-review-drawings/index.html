YOUR DRAWING PACKAGE

What a plan-review set shows.

Each agency sets its own list, so the sheet list follows your state’s rule and the reviewer’s checklist. This list of parts comes from the state rules and the CDC model code cited below.

Request a full package or help with existing plans.

Related: Drawings for your engineer of record · Outsourced pool drafting · Rooftop & elevated pool drawings · Vanishing-edge pool drawings

  1. 01

    Site plan

    The pool on the property, with nearby buildings, walkways, utilities and the equipment room. Some rules also ask for water, sewer and drainage locations on this sheet.

  2. 02

    Pool plan and sections

    A dimensioned plan of each pool and cross sections through it. They show depths, the waterline, inlets, main drains, suction outlets, gutters or skimmers, stairs and ladders.

  3. 03

    Recirculation schematic

    A flow diagram of pumps, filters, chemical feeders, heaters, surge tank, valves, gauges and flow meters. It also shows make-up water and backwash disposal, with pipe sizes taken from your engineer’s design.

  4. 04

    Equipment room and schedules

    The equipment room layout with space for installing and servicing each unit. Schedules list the pumps, filters, feeders and drain covers your engineer selected, drawn from the manufacturer’s data.

  5. 05

    Turnover and bather-load data

    A data sheet with surface area, volume, flow rate, turnover time and bather load or peak occupancy. We enter these figures exactly as your engineer calculated them.

  6. 06

    Deck, barrier and safety items

    Deck paving, slope and drains, the enclosure, gates and latching hardware. Depth markings, signs, safety equipment and accessible entries appear where the rule lists them and your designer places them.

WHO DOES WHAT

Who does what on a public pool submittal.

The drawings record decisions made by the people who are responsible for them.

Ask about your project →
  • You, the owner or developerYou hire the engineer or architect of record and decide which pools, spas and features the project includes. In South Carolina, for example, the owner must sign the construction permit application.
  • Your engineer or architectThey set the design, run the hydraulic and structural calculations, choose the equipment and answer the reviewer on technical points. Where the rule requires a seal, they direct the engineering and seal the set.
  • JEBNWe draft the plans, sections, schematics, schedules and data sheets from your designer’s work. When review comments come back, we redraw the sheets as your engineer directs.
  • The reviewing agencyA state or local health department, a building department or another agency named in your state’s law checks the submittal against its rules. The agency decides approval.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Public pool plan review questions.

Who reviews public pool plans?

It depends on the state. In Washington, the state Department of Health or the local health officer reviews the permit application under WAC 246-260-021. In Iowa, the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing issues the construction permit for pools with more than 500 square feet of water surface. Florida law lets local governments review public pool plans against the Florida Building Code. Check your state’s rule, and ask your local building department whether a separate permit also applies.

Do public pool plans need an engineer’s seal?

It depends on the state, and several state rules require one. Washington asks for plans prepared, stamped and signed by an engineer or architect. Iowa’s rule 481 IAC 485.5(2) requires plans sealed by an engineer or architect licensed in Iowa. South Carolina law, section 44-55-2340, requires plans prepared and signed by an architect or engineer registered in the state. JEBN does not seal drawings, so the seal comes from your design professional.

Can you draft for the project’s engineer of record?

Yes. We draft the set from your engineer’s design, calculations and mark-ups, and we redraw sheets when review comments come back. Where your permit office or health department requires a seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state, or an architect where the rule allows one, must direct the engineering and seal the set. JEBN does not seal drawings.

What does the health department want in the submittal?

Each agency sets its own list. Washington’s and Iowa’s rules both ask for a site or overall plan, a scaled pool plan with cross sections, a piping schematic with pipe sizes and valves, an equipment layout and equipment specifications. Iowa also lists bathhouse drawings. Use your agency’s current checklist as the sheet list, and send it with your quote request so we can lay out the sheets to match the drawing items it lists.

Do you draw to the Model Aquatic Health Code?

The CDC’s Model Aquatic Health Code is voluntary guidance, not federal law. It becomes law only where a state, local, territorial or tribal jurisdiction adopts it, in whole or in part. New Mexico, for example, adopts the first edition by reference with its own changes. Where your jurisdiction uses it, we arrange the sheets around its plan submittal list, and your engineer applies its design requirements.

Do the drawings show drain safety and accessible entry?

The federal Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act requires anti-entrapment drain protection at public pools and spas, including pools open only to hotel guests or to apartment or HOA residents. Where the 2010 ADA Standards apply, a pool needs at least two accessible entries, one a lift or sloped entry. Under 300 linear feet of wall, one lift or sloped entry can be enough. We draw both as your engineer places them.
