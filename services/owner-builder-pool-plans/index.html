YOUR DRAWING PACKAGE

What an owner-builder pool permit set shows.

Each permit office sets its own list, so the sheet list follows yours. An owner-builder set can cover these parts.

Request a full package or help with existing plans.

Get a quote for this set →

Related: Pool permit drawings · How the engineer’s seal works · Site plan and barrier plan · Spa addition & remodel drawings

  1. 01

    Site plan

    The lot drawn to scale with property lines, setbacks, easements, the house and other structures, the pool, deck and equipment pad. Where your checklist asks for drainage, slopes, trees or septic locations, the site plan shows them too.

  2. 02

    Pool plan and depths

    The pool shape, length and width, steps and benches, with depths marked around the edge and wherever they change. The deck layout and dimensions in feet and inches sit on the same sheet.

  3. 03

    Sections through the pool

    Cuts through the walls and floor that show depths, slopes and the build-up of the shell. Steel, concrete or shell details are drawn from your engineer’s design, your builder’s standard details or the shell maker’s data.

  4. 04

    Barrier plan

    Where the fence, gates, house walls or other barrier sit around the pool, with gate swing and the barrier notes your local code calls for. The heights and gaps come from the rules your permit office enforces.

  5. 05

    Plumbing and equipment

    Skimmers, drains, returns and the pipe runs back to the pump, filter and heater, with the equipment pad layout. Pipe sizes and equipment models come from your pool builder, your engineer or your own choices. Manufacturer data sheets can go with the set when your permit office asks for them.

  6. 06

    Electrical and bonding notes

    The locations of pool lights, the pump, heater and equipment pad, with bonding notes taken from your electrician’s details or your permit office’s checklist. Some permit offices issue a separate electrical permit for this work.

WHO DOES WHAT

Who does what on an owner-builder pool project.

As an owner-builder you stand in the contractor’s place, so it helps to know where each task sits.

Ask about your project →
  • YouYou sign the permit application as the owner-builder and take responsibility for the work. You hire and supervise any trades, book the inspections and get failed work corrected and inspected again.
  • Your engineerWhere your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set. You can bring your own engineer to the project.
  • JEBNWe draw the permit set from what you send and revise the sheets when you, your engineer or the permit office mark changes. JEBN does not seal drawings or act as the engineer of record.
  • Your permit officeIt sets the checklist, asks for the owner-builder paperwork, reviews the set and sends comments. The permit office decides approval and carries out the inspections.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Owner-builder pool permit questions.

Can I pull my own pool permit as a homeowner?

In some states, yes. The rules come from your state and your permit office. Florida’s statute has the owner sign the permit application in person and sign a disclosure statement before the permit is issued. Arizona’s exemption covers work for the owner’s own occupancy, not for sale or rent. California’s contractor board lists more than one owner-builder exemption. One covers owners who do the work themselves, or through their own employees, on a structure on their property that is not intended for sale. Another covers a homeowner improving their main home and has several conditions, including that the owner has lived there for the 12 months before the work is completed.

What drawings does a pool permit need?

Each permit office sets its own list. The published checklists listed under Sources ask for items such as a site plan drawn to scale with setbacks, a pool plan with dimensions and depths, sections, barrier details and equipment information. Some also ask for drainage or grading details, flood zone notes and separate electrical or plumbing permits. Send us your permit office’s checklist and we lay out the sheets to match the drawing items it lists.

Do owner-builder pool plans need an engineer’s stamp?

Sometimes. In Florida, Hillsborough County asks for pool plans signed and sealed by a Florida architect or engineer, and Orange County asks for in-ground pool plans from a Florida engineer or architect. Other places differ. Where your permit office requires a seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state, or an architect where your permit office accepts one, must direct the engineering and seal the set. JEBN does not seal drawings. You can use your own engineer or architect.

Can you draw from my sketch or a pool kit maker’s plans?

Yes. We draw from a hand sketch, a survey, existing plans, or a kit maker’s or shell manufacturer’s standard plans and installation data. Some permit offices work with standard pool plans or the maker’s installation instructions, and some still want site-specific plans, so ask yours first. Where your permit office accepts the maker’s plans, we draw the site-specific sheets that go with them: your lot, where the pool sits, the barrier and the equipment.

What happens if the permit office sends comments?

A plan reviewer may hold the permit and send a list of corrections. Send us the list. We revise the sheets and can mark where each item was answered, which some permit offices ask for when you resubmit. Your quote lists the revisions included. Comments about the engineering go to your engineer, and the permit office decides approval once it reviews the corrected set.

What else can the permit office ask an owner-builder for?

Beyond drawings, offices may ask for an owner-builder disclosure or exemption statement, a copy of your ID or a notarized signature, and a form naming who installs the barrier. Some want health department sign-off for a home on a private well and septic system, a recorded notice of commencement or a signed statement on draining pool water. Some also issue separate electrical, plumbing or gas permits. Read your office’s checklist.
