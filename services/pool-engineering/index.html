WHO DOES WHAT

Four parties, four jobs.

A sealed pool set has more than one author. Knowing who does what saves time and avoids paying twice.

Ask about your project →
  • Your permit officeApplies your state’s law and its building code. It tells you whether your pool needs sealed plans, and it reviews the set.
  • The engineer of recordA Professional Engineer licensed in your state. Directs the engineering, checks the drawings and calculations, and seals them.
  • JEBNDrafts the drawing set to the engineer’s design, your builder’s details and the permit office’s checklist. JEBN does not seal drawings.
  • You or your builderChoose the engineer and agree the fee for the engineering with that engineer.
WHEN A PERMIT CALLS FOR AN ENGINEER

When a pool permit calls for an engineer.

Your state’s law and your permit office’s rules decide whether a pool needs sealed plans. Some permit offices list sealed plans among the documents for a pool permit. Where the ICC building or residential code is adopted, the building official may also ask for documents from a registered design professional when special conditions exist. These are situations to raise with your permit office before drawings start.

Ask about your project →
  • The checklist lists sealed plansThe pool submittal checklist asks for plans or structural calculations signed and sealed by an engineer licensed in your state.
  • Close to a structure or slopeThe pool sits near a house footing, a retaining wall, a property-line wall or a slope.
  • Difficult groundHigh groundwater, expansive clay, fill, or a soils report with design recommendations.
  • Non-standard shellsRaised walls, vanishing edges, attached spas or a pool on a structure.
WITH AN ENGINEER OF RECORD

How the work runs when a seal is needed.

  1. 1

    The engineer takes the project

    An engineer licensed in your state agrees to be the engineer of record. Until then nobody can promise you a sealed set.

  2. 2

    The engineer sets the design

    Shell thickness, steel, the soil basis and the calculations come from the engineer.

  3. 3

    JEBN drafts the set

    We draw the plans, sections and details to that design and to your permit office’s checklist.

  4. 4

    The engineer reviews and seals

    The engineer checks the set, asks for changes, and seals and signs it when satisfied.

Already have plans?

A seal certifies the work shown on the documents it is placed on. Plans sealed by an engineer licensed only in another state are generally not enough where your state requires a seal. If a different engineer adapts an earlier sealed design to your site, the NCEES model rules and the rules of states such as California and Florida treat that as engineering the new engineer must take charge of and seal as their own work. Ask your permit office how it handles standard or repeat designs.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Questions about seals and stamps.

Does JEBN seal or stamp pool drawings?

No. JEBN is a drafting business, not a licensed engineering firm, and does not seal drawings. JEBN prepares the drawings. Where your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set.

Does every pool need an engineer?

Not everywhere. Your state’s law and your permit office’s rules decide. Some permit offices ask for sealed plans with every pool permit, and some ask only for certain pools. Ask your permit office for its pool submittal checklist before drawings start.

Who is the engineer of record?

The term is used for the Professional Engineer who is in charge of a project’s engineering documents and who signs and seals them. Not every state’s rules use the term. Where a seal is required, that engineer generally must hold a license in the state where the pool will be built.

Can I send finished drawings to an engineer for a stamp?

Not simply for a stamp. Engineering board rules in states such as California, Florida, Texas and Washington let an engineer seal only work the engineer did, or work done under the engineer’s responsible charge, which some states call direct supervision. The engineer has to be in charge of the engineering while the drawings are prepared. Some states let an engineer take over earlier work under set rules, so ask the engineer what applies in your state.

How do I find an engineer?

You can use an engineer you or your builder already work with. Your state’s engineering board can confirm that an engineer holds a license. You can also ask us: we can ask an engineer licensed in your state to take your project. That engineer decides whether to take it and sets the fee for the engineering.

Who pays the engineer?

You or your builder, by agreement with the engineer. The engineer sets the fee for the engineering. Ask for it in writing before you commit.
