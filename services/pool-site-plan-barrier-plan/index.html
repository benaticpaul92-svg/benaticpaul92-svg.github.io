YOUR DRAWING PACKAGE

What the site plan and barrier plan show.

On a small lot both can fit on one sheet. Every item is drawn from information you or your pool builder supply and laid out to match your permit office’s list.

Request a full package or help with existing plans.

Get a quote for this set →

Related: Pool permit drawings · Pool construction drawings · Owner-builder pool plans · Spa addition & remodel drawings

  1. 01

    Lot lines and existing buildings

    Property lines with their dimensions, the house, garage, sheds, decks and existing fences, taken from your survey or plat. Existing items and new work are drawn so a reviewer can tell them apart.

  2. 02

    Pool position and setbacks

    The pool and deck outline with dimensioned distances to each property line, to the house and to other structures. Your local rules set the minimum setbacks, and each distance is labeled so the reviewer can check it.

  3. 03

    Easements, utilities and septic

    Recorded easements, overhead and underground power lines, and the well, septic tank, drain field and reserve area where your permit office asks for them. Some counties set minimum distances from wells and septic parts to the pool and deck.

  4. 04

    Equipment pad location

    Pump, filter and heater positions with distances to lot lines and structures. Where the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC) applies, equipment outside the barrier must be at least 36 inches from the outside of the barrier.

  5. 05

    Fence line, height and gates

    The barrier drawn around the pool area with its height and type, and each gate marked with its swing direction, self-closing and self-latching hardware and latch height. Notes cite the code section or state law your permit office names.

  6. 06

    House doors, windows and covers

    Doors and windows in house walls that open to the pool area, each marked with the protection you or your pool builder chose. Options can include alarms, self-closing and self-latching doors, or a safety cover, where your code or state law lists them.

WHO DOES WHAT

Who does what on a site and barrier plan.

A barrier plan involves several people, and each one has a separate job.

Ask about your project →
  • YouYou choose the fence, gates and safety features within what your state and local rules allow. You also send the survey or plat and any HOA conditions.
  • Your pool builderYour pool builder sets the pool position, the equipment pad and the fence product, and tells us the gate and door hardware they plan to install.
  • JEBNWe draft the site plan and barrier plan from the documents you send, label every distance, gate and opening, and update the sheets for review comments within the revisions in your quote.
  • Your permit officeYour permit office decides which code edition and local rules apply, reviews the plans, and inspects the barrier and safety features before final approval.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Site plan and barrier plan questions.

What does a pool site plan have to show?

It depends on your permit office’s checklist. The checklists listed under Sources ask for items such as the property lines, the house and other structures, the pool and deck with distances to each lot line, easements and the equipment location. Some also want power lines, wells and septic systems. We draw the items your office lists, to scale and fully dimensioned, so a reviewer can check every distance.

How tall must the pool fence be?

The code and local rules your permit office enforces decide. Where the 2024 ISPSC is adopted, the top of the barrier must be at least 48 inches above grade, measured on the side away from the pool. California’s enclosure option calls for at least 60 inches. Florida’s law calls for at least 4 feet on the outside. Arizona’s statute calls for at least five feet, though some local ordinances apply there instead.

Do I need a survey?

It depends on your permit office. Some ask for a survey of the property showing the pool location, and some want it signed and sealed by a professional surveyor. If your office accepts a plat or an existing site plan, we can draw from that instead. We do not survey the lot, so the distances on our plan come from the documents you send.

Can the house wall be part of the barrier?

It can be, with conditions. Where the 2024 ISPSC applies, doors and operable windows with a sill under 48 inches that open to the pool need protection, such as UL 2017 alarms, an ASTM F1346 safety cover, or another approved means like self-closing, self-latching doors. Florida’s law allows a house wall with no door or window opening to the pool. California’s enclosure option must separate the pool from the home. The barrier plan marks each opening.

Can a safety cover or alarm count instead of a fence?

In some places it can. California asks new or remodeled pools at a single-family home to have two of seven listed features, and Florida asks for at least one of five. A safety cover and alarms are on both lists, but in California a safety cover and an in-pool alarm together do not count as the two features. Rules differ by state and city, so your permit office decides what counts at your address.

Does the site plan need a seal?

It depends on your state and permit office. The ISPSC says the code official must ask for documents prepared by a registered design professional where state law requires it, and some counties ask for sealed pool plans. Where your permit office requires a seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state, or an architect where your permit office accepts one, must direct the engineering and seal the set. JEBN does not seal drawings. You can use your own engineer or architect.
