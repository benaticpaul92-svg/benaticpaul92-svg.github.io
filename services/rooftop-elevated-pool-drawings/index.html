YOUR DRAWING PACKAGE

What the set shows for a pool on a structure.

Each sheet is drawn over the building’s structural and architectural drawings, so the pool and the structure under it read as one set. When the engineer or architect marks up a sheet, we revise it to match.

Request a full package or help with existing plans.

Get a quote for this set →

  1. 01

    Pool plans and sections

    The pool outline, depths and water level drawn over the building grid, slab edges, beams and columns from the structural set. Sections cut through the pool and the structure below so each layer and its thickness is clear.

  2. 02

    Shell and support details

    Shell walls, floor and edges, and the slab, beams or frame that carry them, drawn as your structural engineer designed them. The member sizes, reinforcement and design loads on the sheets are copied from the engineer’s design.

  3. 03

    Pile or pier layout

    Pile or pier locations, grid dimensions, and cap and grade beam outlines, drawn from the engineer’s foundation design. The engineer decides the type, spacing and capacity of each pile.

  4. 04

    Waterproofing and penetrations

    Membrane layers, terminations and drains, plus every pipe, light and fitting that passes through the shell or slab. These are drawn to the architect’s or waterproofing designer’s specification and the manufacturer’s data.

  5. 05

    Plumbing and equipment room

    Pipe routes from the pool to the equipment room, including runs through the building, drawn as the design team laid them out. The room layout shows pumps, filters, heaters, tanks and access space where the design team placed them.

  6. 06

    Shop drawings for trades

    Fabrication and installation sheets for tile, coping, gutters, steel placing, embeds and equipment. Each one follows the coordinated set and the engineer’s details.

WHO DOES WHAT

Who is responsible for what on a raised pool.

On a pool carried by a structure, the design work and the drawing work sit with different people.

Ask about your project →
  • Your structural engineerDesigns the structure that carries the pool, the shell and any piles or piers, and sets the design loads. Where your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set.
  • Your architectSets the building layout and finishes around the pool. The architect, or a waterproofing consultant on the design team, specifies the waterproofing system.
  • JEBNDrafts the pool sheets, details and shop drawings from the design team’s drawings and mark-ups, and points out any clash we notice between sheets while drawing. JEBN does not design or check the structure and does not seal drawings.
  • Your permit officeReviews the submittal and decides approval. Where the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC) is adopted, pool structure follows the International Building Code (IBC) or International Residential Code (IRC), and a pool over a space below, which the code calls an elevated pool, follows a standard called PHTA 10. Check which code editions your permit office uses.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Questions about rooftop and pile-supported pool drawings.

Can you draw a rooftop pool for our structural engineer?

Yes. We draw the pool plans, sections, support details and equipment room from your engineer’s design and the building’s structural set. The engineer marks up the sheets and we revise them to match. Your quote lists the sheets, the revisions included and the delivery date before you commit. Files come as DWG and PDF, so the engineer can lay them over their own drawings.

Who is responsible for the structure that carries the pool?

The project’s structural engineer designs the structure that carries the pool, the shell and its supports, and the architect or a waterproofing consultant specifies the waterproofing. Where your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set. JEBN does not seal drawings. Our part is drafting to the design team’s drawings and mark-ups.

Do you draw the pile or pier layout?

Yes, as your engineer designed it. We draw pile or pier locations, grid dimensions and cap or grade beam outlines from the foundation design. Where the IBC applies, deep foundations are designed on the basis of a geotechnical investigation, so pile type, spacing and capacity come from the engineer and that report. Where the 2024 ISPSC is adopted, pools in coastal high-hazard areas and coastal A zones must be designed and built to ASCE 24.

Why does the weight of the water matter on a structure?

Water weighs about 62.4 pounds per cubic foot, according to the USGS. So each foot of depth adds about 62.4 pounds on every square foot of pool floor, before the shell, finishes and swimmers are counted. Small changes in depth or water level change the load on the structure. That is why the sheets must show depths, water levels and any surge or balance tank exactly as the design team set them.

What do you need from the design team?

Send the structural framing and foundation sheets for the pool area, the architectural plans and waterproofing specification, the pool layout with depths and edge type, and the equipment list with manufacturer data. If your permit office has a checklist or has sent review comments, send those too. We can start from early drawings and update the sheets as the design team issues revisions.

Do you deliver shop drawings as well?

Yes. We draw tile, coping, gutter, steel placing, embed and equipment sheets that follow the coordinated set, delivered as DWG and PDF. Where the IBC applies and the building official allows an item to be a deferred submittal, its documents go to the registered design professional in responsible charge for review before the building official sees them. Ask your design team how they want shop drawings routed.
