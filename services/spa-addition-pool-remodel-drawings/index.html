YOUR DRAWING PACKAGE

What a spa addition or remodel set shows.

A remodel set shows two pools on the same sheets: the one there today and the one after the work. Every sheet marks what is existing and what is new, so the plan reviewer and the crew can tell them apart.

Request a full package or help with existing plans.

Get a quote for this set →

Related: Pool construction drawings · Plumbing & hydraulic drawings · Site plan and barrier plan · Owner-builder pool plans

  1. 01

    As-built plan of the existing pool

    The pool as it stands, drawn from your field measurements, photos and any old plans. Outline, depths, steps, benches, fittings and the equipment pad are shown, with anything not yet confirmed marked for site verification.

  2. 02

    New work

    The added spa, new steps or benches, a changed wall or a new finish, dimensioned from fixed points on the existing pool. New work is drawn with its own line weight and hatch so it stands apart from what stays.

  3. 03

    Demolition notes

    Which parts of the shell, coping, tile, deck and piping are removed, cut back or kept, as your pool builder sets out. Notes flag what is to be protected and what is to be checked once the old surface is open.

  4. 04

    Plumbing and equipment tie-in

    Where new spa or pool lines join the existing suction, return and equipment piping, with valves and any new equipment your pool builder has chosen shown on the pad. Suction outlets and their covers are shown too, so your pool builder and permit office can see what stays and what is replaced.

  5. 05

    Updated site plan and barrier

    Where the permit office asks, the site plan adds the spa, new deck, equipment and distances to property lines, plus the fences, gates and house doors that form the barrier. Some offices inspect an existing fence or wall for code compliance before they close out the pool permit.

  6. 06

    Sections of new structure

    Sections and details through the spa shell, raised walls or new steps. We draw the structure as your engineer or pool builder designed it, from their sketches, details and mark-ups.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Spa addition and remodel questions.

Do I need a permit to add a spa to my existing pool?

It depends on your permit office. Some permit offices count a spa or hot tub as a pool, and some require permits to alter or repair an existing pool, not only to build a new one. Oakland, California, for example, says altering or repairing a pool needs planning review and building permits, and its pool definition includes hot tubs and spas. Ask your permit office what applies at your address.

Can you draw my existing pool from photos and measurements?

Yes. We draw the as-built plan from your field measurements and use the photos to read them correctly. Photos alone cannot give reliable sizes or depths, so we need measured numbers for the outline, depths, steps and fitting locations. Anything that cannot be measured is marked on the drawing for your pool builder to verify on site.

What if there are no drawings of the old pool?

Then the existing pool is drawn only from what can be measured and seen. Hidden items, such as shell thickness, steel and buried pipe routes, are noted on the set as unknown or to be verified when the work opens them up. Your pool builder or engineer decides how to handle what is found, and we revise the drawings to match.

Will the remodel make me bring my pool barrier up to current code?

It can, and your permit office decides. Pleasanton, California says any permit to remodel a pool or spa at a single-family home requires at least two of seven listed drowning prevention features. Oakland has a similar rule for private pools. In Phoenix, altering an existing pool barrier needs a new permit, and when an addition or remodel changes any part of the barrier, the new work must meet the city’s adopted barrier code. Check with your own permit office.

Who seals the drawings for a spa addition or remodel?

Where your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set. JEBN does not seal drawings. Some offices ask for sealed pool engineering with a pool permit, Boulder County, Colorado among them. You can use your own engineer, and we draw the set from that engineer’s design and mark-ups.

Does the federal pool safety law apply to my backyard spa?

Partly. The Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act sets a standard for pool and spa drain covers made, distributed or sold in the United States. Its rules for anti-entrapment equipment on pools in use apply to public pools and spas, which include pools at hotels, member clubs and multi-family housing. They do not apply to a private backyard spa. State and local codes set their own rules for private pools, so ask your permit office what applies to yours.
