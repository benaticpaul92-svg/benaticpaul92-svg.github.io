YOUR DRAWING PACKAGE

What a vanishing-edge drawing set shows.

Each sheet follows the decisions your engineer or pool builder has already made. The set puts those decisions on paper so the permit office can review them and the crew can build from them.

Request a full package or help with existing plans.

Get a quote for this set →

Related: Pool structural drawings · Plumbing & hydraulic drawings · Rooftop & elevated pool drawings · Spa addition & remodel drawings

  1. 01

    Weir wall and spill edge

    The weir wall is drawn in plan and section with its length, its top elevation and the spill face below it. Wall thickness, steel and footing are drawn from your engineer’s design or your pool builder’s standard details.

  2. 02

    Catch basin

    The lower basin is drawn with its length, width, depth, water levels and access points, at the capacity your engineer or pool builder has set. The plan shows where the basin sits against the slope, the deck and the pool wall.

  3. 03

    Edge circulation schematic

    The plumbing schematic shows the edge loop on its own: basin suction outlets, edge pump, return lines to the pool, valves and auto-fill, beside the main filtration loop. Where the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC) is adopted, Section 316.2 calls for either a surface skimming system or a perimeter overflow system on a permanent inground residential pool. The schematic shows which one your pool uses.

  4. 04

    Sections and water levels

    Sections cut through the pool wall, weir and basin show the ground behind them and the water levels at rest and running, all from one datum with the deck and coping.

  5. 05

    Waterproofing and tile details

    Large-scale details show the waterproofing line across the weir top, down the spill face and inside the basin, with the tile, edge cap and the turn into the coping. They are drawn from the manufacturer’s data and your pool builder’s details.

  6. 06

    Equipment layout

    The pad plan places the edge pump next to the filtration equipment, with pipe runs, valves and the pipe and pump sizes your engineer or pool builder selected. Each pump is labeled with the loop it serves.

WHO DOES WHAT

Who decides what on a vanishing-edge pool.

On this kind of pool the design decisions and the drawing work sit with different people. The set should make that plain.

Ask about your project →
  • You and your pool builderYou choose the edge location, its length and the finishes. Your pool builder brings the standard details, the equipment choices and the way the job will be built.
  • Your engineer or pool builderThe weir wall structure, the basin capacity and the edge pump selection are design decisions for your engineer, or for your pool builder where the job has no engineer. Where your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set.
  • JEBNWe draw the plans, sections, water levels, plumbing schematic and edge details from those decisions and deliver DWG and PDF files. JEBN does not seal drawings.
  • Your permit officeYour permit office may have its own checklist for pool submittals and may send review comments on the set. The permit office decides approval.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Questions about vanishing-edge pool drawings.

What extra drawings does a vanishing-edge pool need?

A vanishing-edge set adds the weir wall in plan and section, the catch basin with its water levels, sections cut through the edge, and a plumbing schematic that shows the edge circulation as its own loop. A set can also include waterproofing and tile details at the spill face and an equipment layout with the edge pump. Your quote lists the sheets, the revisions included and the delivery date before you commit.

Who decides how big the catch basin is?

Your engineer or your pool builder decides the basin capacity. JEBN does not size the basin. We draw it at the size they give us. Where the 2024 ISPSC is adopted, Section 316.4.1 sets a minimum surge capacity where perimeter surface skimming systems are used. Your engineer and your permit office decide whether and how that applies to your edge.

Does a vanishing-edge pool need an engineer?

It depends on your state and your permit office. Where the 2024 ISPSC is adopted, Section 107.1 has the code official require documents prepared by a registered design professional where state law calls for one. Where your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in your state must direct the engineering and seal the set. JEBN does not seal drawings. You can use your own engineer.

Can you draw from our structural engineer’s sketches?

Yes. Send the sketches, calculations or mark-ups and we draw the weir wall, the basin walls and the steel as your engineer shows them. If a dimension or level is missing, or two sketches disagree, we send you the question instead of filling the gap ourselves. Your engineer stays in charge of the design and can send mark-ups at any stage. We update the sheets to match, within the revisions in your quote.

Do you draw the edge details for the tile and coping trades?

Yes. We draw the spill face, the weir top, the edge cap or coping, the tile layout at the edge and the waterproofing line, at a scale the trades can work from. The details follow your pool builder’s standard details and the manufacturer’s data for the tile, membrane and edge materials. You get DWG and PDF files, so the trades can take dimensions from the native drawing.

Which barrier and drain rules apply to a vanishing-edge pool?

The 2024 ISPSC defines a vanishing edge, or negative edge, as water flowing over a pool wall into a catch basin. Where that code is adopted, Section 801.2 says a permanent inground pool at a home must meet Chapter 3, including the barrier rules in Section 305 and suction entrapment avoidance in Section 311. Federal rules at 16 CFR part 1450 also cover pool and spa drain covers. Check your state and local code.
