CALIFORNIA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for California.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

City of San Diego

City of San Diego Development Services Department

  • Information Bulletin 712 (June 2021) covers private pools, spas and hot tubs at single-family homes and duplexes. Public pools go to County Environmental Health.
  • Pools may use a city pre-approved master plan or be a custom pool. A custom pool needs construction plans, specifications and structural calculations.
  • The site plan must be signed and stamped by a California registered civil or structural engineer, or a California licensed architect of record.
  • Custom pool plan sheets and the first page of the structural calculations must be stamped by the California civil or structural engineer in responsible charge.
  • Depending on pool depth and design, a geotechnical report by a California registered geotechnical or soils engineer may be required.
  • The bulletin cites SDMC Section 145.3110 for pool setbacks, with a three-foot minimum yard where the pool projects three feet or less above grade.

Source: Information Bulletin 712: How to Obtain a Permit for Private Swimming Pools, Spas, Hot Tubs (City of San Diego)

City of San Jose

City of San Jose Development Services Permit Center

  • Any pool or structure holding water over 18 inches deep and meant for swimming or bathing needs a building permit. This includes in-ground pools, above-ground pools, spas and wading pools.
  • The city page cites Municipal Code Chapter 20.30.500 and California Residential Code Appendix AX for single-family and duplex pools.
  • New or remodeled pools at single-family properties need a full enclosure at least 60 inches high plus at least two more drowning-prevention features.
  • Pool setbacks are measured from the property line to the water edge: 30 feet front, 5 feet rear, 5 feet interior side, and 9 feet on a street-facing corner side.
  • Remodels with a building permit must upgrade suction outlets to anti-entrapment grates meeting ANSI/APSP-16 or its successor.
  • The page lists seven inspections, including underground electrical, underground plumbing, pre-gunite, potting, pre-deck, pre-plaster and final.

Source: Swimming Pools, Spas & Hot Tubs (City of San Jose, updated 04/15/2026)

City of Sacramento

City of Sacramento Community Development Department, Building DivisionOnline portal: Accela Citizen Access (City of Sacramento)

  • Residential pool plan review submittals and resubmittals for one- and two-family dwellings must be made electronically through the city's Accela portal.
  • A complete submittal is three files: application documents, supporting documents and plans. Forms include the CDD-0200 application and the CDD-0213 electrical load worksheet.
  • The plans must include a scaled site plan showing the pool and equipment against the house, property lines and the landside toe of any flood control levee.
  • The CDD-0257 Swimming Pool Requirements must be placed on a plan sheet. Gunite pools need a structural steel schedule and details; fiberglass pools need the prefabricated pool specification.
  • The city requires a 20-foot minimum setback from the landside toe of any flood control levee, and no pool within a public water or sewer easement or within six feet of city water, sewer or drainage pipe.
  • No pool may sit in the required front setback, and no pool may be within three feet of a property line, building or other structure.

Source: CDD-0282 Residential Swimming Pool Plan Review Submittal Requirements (rev. 01-01-2026) · CDD-0257 Private Swimming Pool Requirements (rev. 03-11-2026) · Accela Citizen Access, City of Sacramento

Riverside County (unincorporated)

Riverside County Transportation and Land Management Agency (TLMA), Building and SafetyOnline portal: PLUS Online

  • Residential pool plans need a site plan showing property lines, easements, overhead utilities, structures, the pool shape and all pool equipment, dimensioned to property lines and structures.
  • The county requires a signed Barrier Agreement Letter and at least two compliant barriers, installed, inspected and approved before plastering or filling with water. It cites SB 442 and Riverside County Ordinance 421.
  • The primary barrier option includes a wall or fence at least 5 feet high around the property, with compliant gates and exit door alarms.
  • The Pool Permits page lists a four-stage inspection sequence, with someone required to be home for both barrier inspections to verify alarms.

Source: Residential Swimming Pool, Spa, Hot Tub, and Barrier Plan Check Requirements, form 284-004 rev. 5/2024 (Riverside County) · Pool Permits (Riverside County Building and Safety) · Online Submittal (Riverside County Building and Safety)

San Diego County (unincorporated)

County of San Diego Planning & Development Services, Building Division

  • PDS 379 (rev. 02/09/2026), Swimming Pool and Spa Requirements, is formatted as a plan sheet labelled PS.
  • It applies to new or modified pools at one- or two-dwelling properties in the unincorporated area. Public pools are regulated by the Department of Environmental Health.
  • Pools need an enclosure at least 60 inches high, plus at least one safety feature accepted by the county and one accepted by the State of California, with entrapment avoidance.
  • The document says all enclosure, safety and entrapment measures must be installed, inspected and approved before plastering or filling with water.
  • New or remodeled pools and spas heated for the first time may not use gas heating as the primary source, with listed exceptions.

Source: PDS 379 Swimming Pool and Spa Requirements (County of San Diego, rev. 02/09/2026)

City of Fresno

City of Fresno Planning & Development Department, Development Services DivisionOnline portal: Accela Citizen Access

  • The single-family residential submittal checklist lists swimming pools among the project types it covers.
  • Where a Planning Zone Clearance is required, the application goes in through the Accela Citizen Access portal.
  • Projects that do not need a Zone Clearance get an Ok to Submit stamp from Planning (Planning@fresno.gov) before going to the Building Division for plan check.
  • The checklist asks for a site plan with scope of work, project data, structures with dimensions, setbacks from property lines and easements, and fence height and material.

Source: Submittal Requirements for Single Family Residential Projects (City of Fresno)

CALIFORNIA QUESTIONS

Questions about California pool plans.

How many safety features does California require?

The Swimming Pool Safety Act requires drowning-prevention features from a defined list, and the count has changed with subsequent amendments. We design to the requirement in force in your jurisdiction at the time of submittal.

Do commercial pools follow a different path?

Yes. Public pools run through CBC Chapter 31B and Title 22 with health-department review, which is separate from the residential path.

How fast can you prepare California pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in California?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
