DELAWARE · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Pool permit notes for Kent County, Milford, Newark and more in Delaware.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on November 15, 2026 unless a card gives a later date. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your request. Where a source says “copy saved”, the note was read from a dated copy of that official page.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the quote form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Jump to: Kent County Milford Newark New Castle County

Kent County (Dover area, unincorporated) pool permit notes

Kent County Department of Planning Services, Building Permits
  • A permit is required for above-ground and in-ground pools, hot tubs and spas.
  • The county publishes a pool permit guide and a Pool Barrier Requirements Brochure on its building permit information page.
2 more permit notes
  • Kent County lists the 2018 International Residential Code with county amendments as adopted, and its page names no swimming pool code edition.
  • Fences over 7 feet need a permit except when enclosing a swimming pool area; zoning rules still apply.

Source: What Information Do I Need for a Building Permit - Kent County · Permitting Requirements and Adopted Codes - Kent County

Milford pool permit notes

City of Milford Building Inspections & Permitting

Online portal: Civic Access portal

  • The division handles building permits and inspections for residential work, and its page lists fee schedules and guidelines for pools among other project types.
  • Building permits, inspection scheduling and licenses are handled through the city's Civic Access portal.
1 more permit note
  • The page references the 2018 International Building Code, International Residential Code and International Swimming Pool and Spa Code.

Source: Building Inspections & Permitting - Milford

Newark pool permit notes

City of Newark Code Enforcement Division

Online portal: City self-service portal (EnerGov)

  • The city's Forms and Applications page directs building permit applicants to its online self-service portal.
  • The page lists a downloadable Building Permit Application under the Code Enforcement Division; it has no pool-specific permit page.

Source: Forms and Applications - Newark, Delaware

New Castle County (unincorporated) pool permit notes

New Castle County Department of Land Use

Online portal: eServices (eApply and ePlans)

  • The county says a permit is required for all pools, spas and hot tubs that are 24 inches and above in height or are designed and manufactured to be connected to a circulation system, when they are intended for swimming, bathing or wading.
  • The county's pool permit requirements sheet says in-ground pool drawings must meet ISPSC standards, particularly for minimum depths to support diving boards, ladders and stairs. In-ground pools also need a Lines and Grades plan and the latest Residential L&G Checklist, both signed and sealed by a Delaware licensed engineer or surveyor. The plan is to include any pool heater, overhead wires with any relocation shown, a plan for excess material disposal, the pool fence, construction equipment access and all drainage and sewer easements.
2 more permit notes
  • The same sheet says a private swimming pool may be in the rear yard if the pool and all appurtenances, such as decking, the pool filter and pump, are at least six (6) feet from any side or rear property line. All pools must be at least ten (10) feet horizontally from overhead wires, and no pool or hot tub is permitted in any easement or building setback lines.
  • The county's code page lists the 2024 International Residential Code and the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code as effective 1/1/26. It says Chapter 6 of the County Code and its appendices contain amendments to the adopted codes.

Source: Swimming Pools & Hot Tubs (New Castle County) (copy saved March 7, 2025) · Building Permit Requirements: Swimming Pool & Hot Tub/Spa (New Castle County Department of Land Use, rev. 06/16/2025) (copy saved October 18, 2025) · County Laws & Code (New Castle County) (copy saved August 3, 2026) · Checked November 25, 2026

DELAWARE REQUIREMENTS

What Delaware permit offices check.

Each city and county reviews pool plans against its own checklist. These are requirements and site conditions that can apply in Delaware. Structural design, including any wind, flood, seismic or uplift loads at your site, comes from your engineer or your builder’s standard details. We draw it.

  1. 01Pool barrier, gate and alarm details to the Delaware requirements that apply to your site
  2. 02Site plans and setbacks prepared for Sussex County (Rehoboth Beach, Lewes), New Castle County (Wilmington) and Kent County (Dover) submittals
  3. 03High groundwater in coastal plain sands causing hydrostatic uplift on shells
  4. 04FEMA coastal flood zones along the Atlantic beaches and inland bays
  5. 05Seasonal frost and freeze-thaw on decks, coping and shallow piping
  6. 06Pump head (TDH) calculation sheet where the office asks for one, equipment pad and bonding details

Delaware state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so name your permit office in your request.

Residential code
No statewide residential code. New Castle, Kent and Sussex counties and municipalities may adopt their own building codes under 16 Del. C. ch. 76, with a statewide energy code minimum.
Pool barriers
Set by county and municipal building codes and ordinances. No state pool barrier statute.
Contractor licensing
No state trade license for pool contractors. Contractors must hold a Division of Revenue contractor business license under 30 Del. C. 2502, with plumbing and electrical work done by state-licensed trades.
Public pools
Delaware Division of Public Health, 16 Del. Admin. Code 4464 Public Swimming Pools, which requires plan submission and an Approval to Construct before building or altering a public pool.
Engineer licensing
Delaware Association of Professional Engineers (DAPE)

DELAWARE QUESTIONS

Questions about Delaware pool plans.

Does Delaware have a statewide residential building code?

No. New Castle, Kent and Sussex counties and incorporated towns adopt their own building codes, so pool drawings follow the IRC edition and barrier rules of the permitting county or municipality.

Do public pools in Delaware need state approval before construction?

Yes. Under 16 Del. Admin. Code 4464, plans and specifications go to the Division of Public Health, and an Approval to Construct must be issued before a public pool is built, altered or converted from private use.

Can my Delaware pool plans be sealed by an engineer licensed in Delaware?

Only when a Professional Engineer licensed in Delaware takes your project and directs the engineering while the drawings are prepared. JEBN prepares the drawings and does not seal them.

What if plan review comes back with comments?

Send us the comments. Your quote states the revisions included, and we identify the sheets each comment affects. Comments on the engineering go to the engineer of record.

How fast can you prepare Delaware pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. Where your permit needs an engineer’s seal, the engineer’s work takes additional time, and so does the permit office’s own review.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Delaware?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings that respond to it.
