GEORGIA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Georgia.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Cobb County (unincorporated)

Cobb County Community Development, Development and Inspections Division

  • All in-ground and above-ground pools in unincorporated Cobb County must be permitted and inspected by Cobb County Community Development.
  • The residential checklist requires a proposed pool design signed and sealed by a land surveyor, professional engineer or landscape architect registered in Georgia.
  • Septic approval from Environmental Health is required when the property is on septic, and incomplete packages are not accepted.
  • The permit package is emailed to PoolPermits@cobbcounty.gov rather than filed through an online portal.
  • From 1 January 2026 the pool builder must hold a Georgia general contractor licence or a recognized pool-builder certification.

Source: Pool and Spa Inspections - Cobb County · Pool Permits - Residential Required Documents Checklist (01/2026)

Gwinnett County

Gwinnett County Department of Planning and DevelopmentOnline portal: Gwinnett County ZIP Portal (Accela Citizen Access)

  • The county's residential pool guidance (revised August 2012) requires a scaled Swimming Pool Location Plan approved by the Development Review Section before the building permit is issued.
  • The location plan does not have to be drawn by a surveyor or engineer, but it must be legible and drawn to an engineer's or architect's scale.
  • Pools and equipment must sit in the rear yard, at least 5 feet from side and rear property lines, and may not be in easements; the plan must show 100-year floodplain limits where they apply.
  • Homes on a private septic system need Gwinnett Environmental Health approval of the pool location before the permit can be issued.
  • The guidance requires a barrier at least 4 feet high with self-closing, self-latching, lockable gates and no openings that pass a 4 inch sphere.

Source: Swimming Pool (Residential) Building Permit Requirements (BPS-15) · Swimming Pool Location Plan (SPLP) · Gwinnett County ZIP Portal

DeKalb County (unincorporated)

DeKalb County Department of Planning and SustainabilityOnline portal: e-Permitting Portal

  • Pool permits are filed through the county's e-Permitting Portal, and plans are submitted separately through ProjectDox.
  • A pool permit needs a building permit application and a site plan prepared by an engineer or surveyor registered in Georgia.
  • The county says the site plan may be up to ten years old if it still reflects current site conditions.
  • The county's guide says a pool needs Board of Health approval, appropriate fencing and an electrical permit.
  • Properties inside one of DeKalb's incorporated cities are not covered by the county; the guide applies to unincorporated DeKalb.

Source: Permits, Plan Review & Inspections - DeKalb County · Residential Additions Permit Guide (revised 03/2025)

Fulton County (unincorporated)

Fulton County Planning and Community Services

  • The county's swimming pool permit application states that private pools must be fully enclosed by a fence, wall or building at least 5 feet high with self-closing, positive-latching gates.
  • The same form says pools must be in the side or rear yard, and the pool, equipment and deck must be 10 feet from all property lines.
  • A separate fence permit is not required when the fence is included in the pool permit application.
  • Pools in or next to a Special Flood Hazard Area need an as-built lowest floor elevation certificate from a Georgia professional engineer or surveyor.

Source: Fulton County Swimming Pool Permit Application

Savannah area / Chatham County

Chatham County Department of Building Safety and Regulatory Services

  • A building permit is required for pools involving structural work or supports and for all pools with a permanently equipped water circulating system.
  • The county posts a Pool & Spa Permit Application and accepts applications in person at its Eisenhower Drive office; fax and email submissions are not accepted.
  • Chatham County's permit application guidance excludes pools from the homeowner-performed-work option.

Source: When are Building Permits Required - Chatham County · Building Permit Applications - Chatham County · Applying for a permit - Chatham County

Augusta-Richmond County

Augusta License and Inspection Department

  • The residential permit requirements sheet lists a pool permit package of a signed application, plans showing the scope of work and a plot or site plan.
  • The License and Inspection Department must approve the pool barrier before any water is put in the pool.
  • The city's pool page links a Swimming Pool Memorandum and a Swimming Pool Responsibility Agreement for contractors.
  • Inspections can be requested through the city's online Inspection Request form.

Source: Swimming Pools - Augusta, GA · Residential Building Permit Requirements - Augusta, GA

Georgia state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
2024 IRC with 2026 Georgia Amendments, a mandatory state minimum standard code since Jan 1, 2026, adopted by the Department of Community Affairs and enforced locally.
Pool barriers
Residential barriers follow the 2024 ISPSC with 2026 Georgia Amendments, a mandatory state minimum code since Jan 1, 2026.
Contractor licensing
No pool-specific state license; the State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors licenses residential and general contractors, with local business licensing also applying.
Public pools
Georgia Department of Public Health Rules Chapter 511-3-5, Public Swimming Pools, Spas and Recreational Water Parks, covering permits and structural and dimensional design.
Engineer licensing
Georgia State Board of Registration for Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors

GEORGIA QUESTIONS

Questions about Georgia pool plans.

Which codes apply to Georgia residential pools in 2026?

The 2024 IRC and 2024 ISPSC with Georgia Amendments became mandatory state minimum codes on January 1, 2026. Local jurisdictions enforce them, so drawings should cite the 2024 editions and the 2026 Georgia Amendments.

Which rules govern public pool design in Georgia?

Public pools, spas and water parks fall under Georgia DPH Rules Chapter 511-3-5, which sets permit, structural and dimensional design requirements. Applications are handled through the county environmental health office.

Are your Georgia pool plans sealed by a Georgia-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Georgia reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Georgia jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Georgia pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Georgia?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
