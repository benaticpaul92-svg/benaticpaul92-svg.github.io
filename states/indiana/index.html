Pool permit notes for Indianapolis, Carmel, Westfield and more in Indiana.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on November 15, 2026 unless a card gives a later date. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your request.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the quote form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Indianapolis (Marion County) pool permit notes

Department of Business and Neighborhood Services

Online portal: Accela Citizens Access

  • In-ground and above-ground pools, and hot tubs, need structural, improvement location and drainage permits, like any other structure.
  • An above-ground pool 30 inches or less deep and 15 feet or less across does not need a structural permit. One 18 inches or less deep and 15 feet or less in diameter does not need an improvement location permit.
2 more permit notes
  • A pool may not sit in a front yard, closer to a side lot line than the district's minimum side yard setback, or closer than five feet to a rear lot line.
  • The pool area must be enclosed by a safety pool cover meeting 675 IAC 20-4-27(c), or by a fence or other barrier with a self-closing, self-latching gate.

Source: Residential Development Permits (indy.gov) · Checked November 18, 2026

Carmel pool permit notes

Building and Code Services, Community Services Department

Online portal: Online Permitting, with plan upload through Project Dox

  • Carmel applicants file a residential permit online, then get an email to upload plans to the Project Dox submittal system once a permit number is assigned.
  • The city says residential projects must meet the 2020 Indiana Residential Code, which is the 2018 IRC with the 2020 Indiana amendments.
2 more permit notes
  • Carmel's pool inspection procedures list bonding and grounding and final inspections, with the equipotential bonding grid checked against NEC Section 680.26.
  • For pools without a cover, the same document requires a fence at least 5 feet high with self-closing, self-latching, lockable gates, under a separate fence permit.

Source: Residential Permits - City of Carmel · Swimming Pool Inspection Procedures - City of Carmel Building and Code Services

Westfield pool permit notes

Community Development Department

Online portal: City of Westfield Citizens Portal

  • Westfield lists a Residential Pool (RP) permit for in-ground pools, submitted through its Citizens Portal after registering an account.
  • Required documents include a site or plot plan showing property lines, existing structures, right-of-way and all easements, with scale, north arrow and address or lot number.
2 more permit notes
  • The pool page lists a State Construction Design Release (CDR) and an Erosion Control Application among the documents to prepare.
  • Construction documents must include a pool plan and a cross-section drawing of the pool, and review does not guarantee full compliance with the Indiana Building Code.

Source: Pool Permits - City of Westfield · Apply for a Permit - City of Westfield

Lawrence (Marion County) pool permit notes

City of Lawrence Department of Public Works
  • Lawrence's Department of Public Works says every in-ground pool needs a permit, and above-ground pools need one if over 18 inches deep or over 15 feet across with an electrical connection.
  • The city handout says pool owners must also get an Improvement Location Permit from the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services in Marion County.
2 more permit notes
  • Pool permit applications must include a site plan showing the pool against structures, lot lines, easements and wetland buffers, plus the fence design with gate details.
  • The handout, based in part on the 2020 Indiana Residential Code, bars pools from front yards or within 5 feet of a rear lot line and says the structural design must be engineered.

Source: Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs - City of Lawrence Department of Public Works · Checked November 18, 2026

South Bend pool permit notes (St. Joseph County)

St. Joseph County / City of South Bend Building Department

Online portal: City and County Swimming Pool Permit Application (online form)

  • South Bend's Building Department lists a City and County Swimming Pool Permit Application among its residential permit forms, submitted through an online form.
  • The department's permits page says the 2020 Indiana Residential Code, based on the 2018 International Code, is in effect by state adoption.
1 more permit note
  • Contractors must be registered or licensed with the Building Department. A residential owner-occupant may do their own work on their dwelling.

Source: Building Permits & Contractor Registrations - South Bend, Indiana · Building - South Bend, Indiana · Checked November 18, 2026

Cicero (Hamilton County) pool permit notes

Cicero / Jackson Township Plan Commission
  • Cicero and Jackson Township use a joint Plan Commission Swimming Pool/Spa Permit Application for residential and commercial pools, in-ground and above-ground.
  • The application says all pool and spa installations must follow Title 675 of the Indiana Administrative Code, 675 IAC 20-1 through 20-4.
2 more permit notes
  • The town asks for one copy of the pool construction plans, showing the required 5-foot wall, fence or barrier around the pool, and a site plan with property line dimensions and site improvements.
  • The form lists bonding, electrical and final inspections, and says detached fences, decks and accessory structures need their own permits.

Source: Cicero / Jackson Township Plan Commission Swimming Pool/Spa Permit Application · Checked November 18, 2026

Indiana state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so name your permit office in your request.

Residential code
2020 Indiana Residential Code (675 IAC 14-4.4, based on the 2018 IRC), adopted statewide by the Fire Prevention and Building Safety Commission effective December 26, 2019, with plan review and inspection handled locally.
Pool barriers
Indiana Residential Code Section R326 (675 IAC 14-4.4-38), a state amendment replacing the model pool section, requires a barrier at least 4 ft high around the pool and deck with self-closing, latching gates, or an ASTM F1346 power safety cover.
Contractor licensing
No statewide license for residential pool contractors; Indiana licenses plumbers at state level, and builder licensing is set locally by cities and counties.
Public pools
Public pools, spas and water attractions are built to the Indiana Swimming Pool Code, 675 IAC 20, with design release from the Department of Homeland Security, Division of Fire and Building Safety; operation falls under Indiana Department of Health rule 410 IAC 6-2.1.
Engineer licensing
Indiana State Board of Registration for Professional Engineers

INDIANA QUESTIONS

Questions about Indiana pool plans.

What barrier does Indiana require around a residential pool?

Section R326 of the 2020 Indiana Residential Code requires walls or fencing at least 4 ft high around the pool and deck with self-closing, latching, lockable gates, or a key-operated power safety cover meeting ASTM F1346.

Do public pool plans in Indiana need a design release?

Yes. Public pools and spas need a Construction Design Release from the Division of Fire and Building Safety under 675 IAC 20, with plans sealed by an Indiana registered architect or engineer, before local permits are issued.

Can my Indiana pool plans be sealed by an engineer licensed in Indiana?

Yes. When your permit office requires a seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in Indiana reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies the seal. PE review and sealing is quoted as part of your package, and we confirm the responsible engineer before work begins.

What if plan review comes back with comments?

Send us the comments. Your quote states the revision support included, and we identify the sheets and calculations each comment affects.

How fast can you prepare Indiana pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Indiana?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
