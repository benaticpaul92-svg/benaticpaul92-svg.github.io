IOWA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Iowa.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Ankeny

Community Planning & Building DepartmentOnline portal: Online permit application (Apply For Permit link)

  • Ankeny's pool handout requires a building permit for all swimming pools, a site plan showing the pool, decks, the barrier, easements, drainage, well and septic, and one set of pool construction drawings or manufacturer specifications.
  • The handout requires a detailed drawing of the pool barrier, and separate permits for the fence, electrical, mechanical and plumbing work.
  • Under the city code section quoted in the handout, residential pools go in the side or rear yard, at least 4 ft from any property line, and outdoor pools must be enclosed by a barrier.
  • Ankeny lists the 2024 I-codes with amendments effective July 1, 2025, including its own 2024 Swimming Pool and Spa Code (Municipal Code Chapter 183).

Source: Swimming Pools - Residential Guidelines, City of Ankeny · Building Codes - City of Ankeny · Building Information - City of Ankeny

Davenport

Building & Land DevelopmentOnline portal: E-Plan

  • Davenport takes plans and applications through its E-Plan portal and accepts only unsecured PDF files, with drawings uploaded as individual vector-based PDFs.
  • The city's permit guide exempts prefabricated pools accessory to a Group R-3 occupancy only if they are less than 24 inches deep and not over 5,000 gallons; they must still meet fencing and enclosure rules.
  • Other pools fall under general building improvements and need a building permit.

Source: Electronic Plan Review - City of Davenport · What Does/Does Not Require a Permit? - City of Davenport

Urbandale

Community Development (Building Services)Online portal: Permit Portal

  • Urbandale applicants create an account in its cloud-based Permit Portal to apply, follow the review and track the permit through final inspection.
  • The city's Building Codes page lists the 2018 International Building Code and 2018 International Residential Code.

Source: Permits - Other Residential - City of Urbandale · Building Codes - City of Urbandale

Iowa state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
No mandatory statewide residential code; the Iowa State Building Code (2024 IRC as amended, 481 IAC 301.8) applies to state-regulated buildings and in jurisdictions that adopt it, and other cities and counties adopt their own codes.
Pool barriers
No state statute for private residential pool barriers; barrier requirements are set by the local building code or ordinance.
Contractor licensing
Contractors earning $2,000 or more a year from construction must register with the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing under Iowa Code chapter 91C; there is no pool-specific state license.
Public pools
The Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing enforces 481 IAC chapter 485 (Swimming Pools, Spas, and Spray Pads) under Iowa Code chapter 135I, requiring a construction permit with plans sealed by an Iowa engineer or architect for pools over 500 sq ft.
Engineer licensing
Iowa Engineering and Land Surveying Examining Board

IOWA QUESTIONS

Questions about Iowa pool plans.

Does Iowa have a statewide code for backyard pools?

No. The Iowa State Building Code applies only where adopted or to state-regulated buildings. Residential pool barriers, setbacks and permit submittals follow the city or county code. We confirm the edition in force for your jurisdiction before drawings are issued.

When does a commercial pool in Iowa need state plan approval?

Under 481 IAC 485, a pool with more than 500 sq ft of water surface needs a construction permit from DIAL before work starts, and the plans must be sealed by an engineer or architect licensed in Iowa.

Are your Iowa pool plans sealed by a Iowa-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Iowa reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Iowa jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Iowa pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Iowa?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
