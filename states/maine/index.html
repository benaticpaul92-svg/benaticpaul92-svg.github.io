Maine at a Glance

Maine pool permit requirements

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
Maine Uniform Building and Energy Code (MUBEC) based on the 2021 IRC; it applies statewide, with mandatory local enforcement in municipalities of 4,000 or more residents and optional enforcement in smaller towns.
Pool barriers
No state pool barrier statute; barrier requirements are set by municipal ordinance and local code enforcement.
Contractor licensing
No statewide license for residential pool contractors; Maine licenses plumbers and electricians at state level, but general and pool builders are not state licensed.
Public pools
Maine CDC Health Inspection Program licenses public pools and spas under 10-144 CMR Chapter 202 (public swimming pool rules), with plan review of a registration form, pool plans and equipment specifications before construction.
Engineer licensing
Maine State Board of Licensure for Professional Engineers
Questions

Frequently Asked

Which building code applies to residential pools in Maine?

Maine uses MUBEC, based on the 2021 IRC. Towns with 4,000 or more residents must enforce it; smaller towns may opt out. Pool barrier and setback rules come from the local ordinance, which we check for your town before drawings are issued.

What does Maine CDC need to review a public pool?

The Health Inspection Program asks for a registration form, pool plans showing depths, area, piping and safety features, pump and filter specifications, and the review fee. Allow at least 30 working days for review.

Are your Maine pool plans sealed by a Maine-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Maine reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Maine jurisdiction.
