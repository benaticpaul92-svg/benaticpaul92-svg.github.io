MARYLAND · SHORT ANSWER

Do I need an engineer’s stamp for a pool in Maryland?

In Howard County, yes: all residential swimming pool plans must be signed and sealed by an engineer.

Howard County says so in its September 2025 memo and its pool checklist. Elsewhere in Maryland, ask the building department that issues your permit what it requires. For public pools and spas, COMAR 10.17.01.05 requires plans prepared by a licensed or certified professional engineer, architect, draftsman, or a person with extensive experience in the design of pools and spas. Rules change, so check the current requirements before you submit.

MARYLAND · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Pool permit notes for Montgomery County, Baltimore County, Howard County and more in Maryland.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on November 15, 2026 unless a card gives a later date. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your request. Where a source says “copy saved”, the note was read from a dated copy of that official page.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the quote form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Montgomery County pool permit notes

Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services

Online portal: DPS eServices

  • A building permit is required to build, alter or enlarge any residential swimming pool, above or below ground. An electrical permit is also required.
  • Plans are submitted electronically through the DPS eServices system. They must be drawn to scale and include a site plan, the pool layout with all equipment, the piping layout, structural sections and a finish schedule.
2 more permit notes
  • Pools installed after January 1, 1990 must be protected by a barrier 60 inches tall. Older pools need a fence at least 42 inches tall or an automatic pool cover.
  • Properties with an individual well and septic system have additional requirements. The county does not enforce private covenants and deed restrictions.

Source: Residential Swimming Pool (Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services) · Checked November 18, 2026

Baltimore County pool permit notes

Baltimore County Department of Permits, Approvals and Inspections
  • All in-ground pools need a permit and a fence built to code. Above-ground pools over 250 square feet with a water depth of 24 inches or more also need a permit.
  • If the pool is in a CBCA, flood plain or historical property, a permit is required regardless of size.
2 more permit notes
  • Residential applications need a pool letter signed by the property owner and site plans. Where the property has a well and septic system, the site plan must follow the county's Ground Water Site Plan Requirements.
  • A plumbing permit is also needed when a permanent water line will fill the pool. Applications are made through the county's online permit portal.

Source: Swimming Pools Building Permit (Baltimore County Government) (copy saved December 24, 2025) · Checked November 18, 2026

Howard County pool permit notes

Howard County Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits
  • A plan review memo effective September 7, 2025 applies to all residential swimming pool applications. It states that all plans must be signed and sealed by an engineer.
  • In-ground pool applications need a plot/site plan and verification of compliance with the county checklist based on Chapter 8 of the International Swimming Pool & Spa Code. A certificate of compliance is required at inspection.
2 more permit notes
  • Above-ground pools need a plot/site plan and the manufacturer's installation guidelines, and also a certificate of compliance at inspection.
  • The county checklist asks for pool shape, wall cross section, floor slope, diving equipment and diving envelope where applicable. Construction plans should reference the current International Swimming Pool & Spa Code.

Source: Requirements for submittal of all residential swimming pool applications (Howard County memo, 2 Sept 2025) · Checklist for Residential Swimming Pool Submittal (Howard County)

Anne Arundel County pool permit notes

Anne Arundel County Inspections and Permits

Online portal: Land Use Navigator

  • The county lists a pool among residential accessory structures that may need a building permit.
  • Applications, status checks and inspection requests run through the Land Use Navigator system.
2 more permit notes
  • Submittals include a building permit application, construction plans to a recognized architectural scale, and a site plan at an engineered scale. The site plan must mark easements, property lines, rights of way and any grinder pump, well or septic system.
  • Other attachments, such as a Critical Area Worksheet and a Standard Grading Plan Application, are listed on the page and may not apply to every project.

Source: Residential Accessory Structure (Anne Arundel County)

Carroll County pool permit notes

Carroll County Bureau of Permits and Inspections
  • The county says a swimming pool needs both a building permit and an electrical permit. A hot tub or spa needs only an electrical permit.
  • Pools must comply with the 2021 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC) and Carroll County amendments.
2 more permit notes
  • The pool site must be approved by the Carroll County Zoning Administration and the Health Department, to keep it far enough from septic systems and water infrastructure.
  • The plot plan of the whole parcel, drawn to scale, must show the well or septic area, drywell, road name and all easements (floodplain, forest conservation, utilities), plus the area of disturbance. The pool contractor signs a Pool Enclosure Affidavit.

Source: Installing a Swimming Pool, Spa, or Hot Tub (Carroll County) · Checked November 18, 2026

Maryland state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so name your permit office in your request.

Residential code
2021 IRC with state amendments under the Maryland Building Performance Standards (COMAR 09.12.51, Maryland Department of Labor), effective May 29, 2023. Local jurisdictions must enforce it and may amend.
Pool barriers
Maryland Swimming Pool and Spa Standards (Public Safety Article §12-1201 et seq., COMAR 09.12.56) adopt the 2021 ISPSC with state amendments for pools with permit applications received on or after December 31, 2023.
Contractor licensing
Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC) license. Business Regulation Article §8-101 counts construction of a swimming pool on land adjacent to a residence as home improvement.
Public pools
Maryland Department of Health, COMAR 10.17.01 Public Swimming Pools and Spas. Construction, alteration and replacement applications go to MDH unless the work is in Anne Arundel, Baltimore, Howard, Montgomery or Prince George’s County or Baltimore City.
Engineer licensing
Maryland State Board for Professional Engineers

MARYLAND QUESTIONS

Questions about Maryland pool plans.

Which pool code applies to a residential pool permit in Maryland?

Pools with permit applications received on or after December 31, 2023 must meet the Maryland Swimming Pool and Spa Standards, COMAR 09.12.56, which adopt the 2021 ISPSC with state amendments. The county or city enforces it.

Does a Maryland residential pool builder need a state license?

Yes. Building a pool adjacent to a residence is home improvement under Business Regulation Article §8-101, so the contractor needs a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license. Public pools need a separate construction or alteration permit under COMAR 10.17.01.05. Applications go to the Maryland Department of Health unless the work is in Anne Arundel, Baltimore, Howard, Montgomery or Prince George’s County or Baltimore City.

Can my Maryland pool plans be sealed by an engineer licensed in Maryland?

Only when a Professional Engineer licensed in Maryland takes your project and directs the engineering while the drawings are prepared. JEBN prepares the drawings and does not seal them.

What if plan review comes back with comments?

Send us the comments. Your quote states the revision support included, and we identify the sheets each comment affects. Comments on the engineering go to the engineer of record.

How fast can you prepare Maryland pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. Where your permit needs an engineer’s seal, the engineer’s work takes additional time, and so does the permit office’s own review.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Maryland?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings that respond to it.
