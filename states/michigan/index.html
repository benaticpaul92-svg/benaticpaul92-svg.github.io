MICHIGAN · SHORT ANSWER

Do I need an engineer’s stamp for a pool in Michigan?

For public pool work costing $15,000 or more, yes: the plans must bear the seal of an engineer or architect licensed in Michigan.

Michigan rule R 325.2113 requires plans prepared by, and bearing the seal of, a professional engineer or architect licensed in Michigan when a new public pool and related facilities, or a modification of one, cost $15,000 or more. EGLE, the state environment department, says every new or modified public pool needs a construction permit. For a home pool, ask the building department that issues your permit what it requires. Rules change, so check the current requirements before you submit.

JEBN does not seal drawings. If your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in Michigan must direct the engineering and seal the set. How the engineer’s seal works

MICHIGAN · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Pool permit notes for Grand Rapids, Sterling Heights, Detroit and more in Michigan.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on November 15, 2026 unless a card gives a later date. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your request. Where a source says “copy saved”, the note was read from a dated copy of that official page.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the quote form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Grand Rapids pool permit notes (Kent County)

Development Center (City of Grand Rapids)

Online portal: Citizen Access (Accela)

  • Grand Rapids requires a building permit for a pool or hot tub that can reach 24 inches of water depth or more.
  • The pool permit page asks for a pool installation plan and a site plan showing the pool location on the lot, uploaded with the application.
2 more permit notes
  • A non-owner applicant needs a Residential Builders License issued by the State of Michigan, or written authorization from a license holder.
  • The city points applicants to its Residential Pool Guide and its zoning rules for placement.

Source: Pool Permit (City of Grand Rapids) · Overview on Zoning and Permits for Home Renovation Projects · Residential Building Permits (City of Grand Rapids)

Sterling Heights pool permit notes (Macomb County)

Office of Building, City of Sterling Heights

Online Permits (Building)

  • Sterling Heights' pool guide covers pools and hot tubs with side walls over 24 inches. It lists a building permit application, plus electrical and mechanical permits where that work applies.
  • Pool plans must show the location on the property, with the water’s edge at least 6 feet from side and rear lot lines and easements.
2 more permit notes
  • The plans must also show overhead wires, the electric meter and service line, barrier or enclosure details, and entrapment protection for a pool with a floor drain.
  • Inspections listed for an in-ground pool include a building open hole inspection and a final building inspection, with electrical inspections if electric is installed.

Source: Pool Permit Packet and Pool & Hot Tub Guide (Sterling Heights) · Online Permits (Sterling Heights)

Detroit pool permit notes (Wayne County)

Buildings, Safety Engineering and Environmental Department (BSEED)

Online portal: eLAPS (Accela Citizen Access)

  • BSEED says building permits are applied for in the Accela portal, with scope of work and plans submitted through the ProjectDox portal.
  • BSEED’s FAQ says single-family and duplex residential structures may apply in the office.
2 more permit notes
  • The Detroit Property Maintenance Code (Sec. 9-1-109) says pools must be fully enclosed by a fence at least 4 feet high with a self-latching gate.
  • The same section says pools must be installed consistent with the Michigan Construction Codes and have a working water filtration device.

Source: BSEED Frequently Asked Questions · Detroit Property Maintenance Code, Chapter 9 (Sec. 9-1-109 Swimming pools)

Ann Arbor pool permit notes (Washtenaw County)

Building, Rental and Inspection Services; Planning and Development Services

Online portal: STREAM

  • Ann Arbor takes building permit applications through its STREAM online portal, which handles applications filed since March 2022.
  • Electrical, mechanical and plumbing trade permits are also applied for in STREAM and are not released until the related building permit is approved.
1 more permit note
  • The city’s accessory building handout lists pools among accessory structures. Zoning compliance permits for such structures must be submitted online in STREAM.

Source: Applications (Ann Arbor Building, Rental and Inspection Services) · Permits (Ann Arbor Building, Rental and Inspection Services) · Zoning Compliance Permits (Ann Arbor) · Accessory Buildings handout (Ann Arbor Planning)

Rochester Hills pool permit notes (Oakland County)

City of Rochester Hills Building Department

Online portal: BS&A Online

  • The city’s pool guidebook says a building permit is required for a residential pool, hot tub or spa that has any one of these: a depth intended for swimming or bathing of more than 24 inches, a permanent water-recirculation system, or construction that involves structural materials. The construction documents are reviewed against the Michigan Residential Code and the International Swimming Pool and Spa Code.
  • The guidebook says pools, hot tubs and spas must be at least 5 feet from the side and rear property lines and 10 feet from the home, measured from the water’s edge, and must not be located in any front yard.
2 more permit notes
  • The guidebook says the pool fence or barrier must be 48 inches above grade, measured on the side facing away from the pool, and pedestrian gates must open outward away from the pool and be self-closing with a self-latching device. A pool, hot tub or spa must not be filled with water until the Michigan Residential Code barrier requirements are completed.
  • The city’s pool permit application guide lists the construction drawings for the pool, a site plan signed and sealed by a State of Michigan licensed design professional, and the completed City of Rochester Hills Pool Information Form as required attachments, and says mechanical, electrical and plumbing permits can be applied for online after the building permit is issued.

Source: A Guide to Residential Swimming Pool, Hot Tub and Spa Construction (City of Rochester Hills, September 15, 2026) · Residential Swimming Pool, Hot Tub and Spa Permit Application Guide (City of Rochester Hills) · Apply for a Permit Online (City of Rochester Hills) (copy saved August 14, 2026) · Checked November 25, 2026

Michigan state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so name your permit office in your request.

Residential code
2015 Michigan Residential Code (2015 IRC basis, LARA Bureau of Construction Codes) remains the current statewide code. The 2021 MRC was filed in 2025 but stayed by the Michigan Court of Claims in July 2025.
Pool barriers
Residential barriers follow the 2015 ISPSC Section 305 referenced from the 2015 Michigan Residential Code, enforced by the local building department, which may add pool ordinances.
Contractor licensing
LARA Bureau of Construction Codes licenses residential builders and maintenance and alteration contractors. Swimming pools is a listed M&A trade.
Public pools
Michigan EGLE construction permit under Part 125 of the Public Health Code (1978 PA 368) and the public swimming pool rules, Mich. Admin. Code R 325.2111 et seq. (plans and permit application at R 325.2113).
Engineer licensing
Michigan Board of Professional Engineers

MICHIGAN QUESTIONS

Questions about Michigan pool plans.

Which residential code edition applies to Michigan pool permits right now?

The 2015 Michigan Residential Code. The 2021 edition was filed in 2025 but stayed by the Michigan Court of Claims. We confirm the edition in force for your jurisdiction before drawings are issued.

When do Michigan public pool plans need an engineer’s or architect’s seal?

Plans must be prepared by, and bear the seal of, a professional engineer or architect licensed in Michigan when a new public pool and its related facilities, or a modification of one, cost $15,000 or more (R 325.2113). EGLE, the state environment department, also requires a construction permit for every new public pool and every modification of an existing one. Construction may begin once EGLE assigns the permit number and date, and once the applicable local construction permits are obtained.

Can my Michigan pool plans be sealed by an engineer licensed in Michigan?

Only when a Professional Engineer licensed in Michigan takes your project and directs the engineering while the drawings are prepared. JEBN prepares the drawings and does not seal them.

What if plan review comes back with comments?

Send us the comments. Your quote states the revisions included, and we identify the sheets each comment affects. Comments on the engineering go to the engineer of record.

How fast can you prepare Michigan pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. Where your permit needs an engineer’s seal, the engineer’s work takes additional time, and so does the permit office’s own review.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Michigan?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings that respond to it.
