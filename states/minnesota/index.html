MINNESOTA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Minnesota.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Saint Paul / Ramsey County

Department of Safety and Inspections (DSI)Online portal: Online Permit System (Tyler Portico)

  • DSI's When Is a Permit Required guide says in-ground and above-ground pools over 5000 gallons need a building permit, and grading or filling for an in-ground pool needs a grading permit.
  • DSI's pool handout under Zoning Code Section 65.923 sets in-ground pools at least 5 feet from the adjoining property line and 4 feet from any building on the lot.
  • The same handout requires yards with a pool at a one- or two-family home to have an obscuring fence at least 4 feet high, with gaps no larger than 4 inches and self-closing, self-latching gates.
  • New pool wiring needs a separate electrical permit and must meet National Electrical Code Article 680.

Source: Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs (DSI handout) · When Is a Permit Required? (DSI) · Building Permits & Inspections (DSI)

Maple Grove / Hennepin County

Building Department, City of Maple Grove

  • Maple Grove's residential pool handout says a permit is required for pools and hot tubs valued over $500, and any pool over 18 inches deep or 150 square feet must follow its rules regardless.
  • Plans must include a site plan showing the pool relative to structures, lot lines, easements and wetland buffers, plus fence and gate design details.
  • The handout says pools must be at least 5 feet from lot lines, may not extend into the front yard setback, and must be engineered to withstand the expected forces.
  • Permanent fences must be at least 4 feet high with self-closing, self-latching gates, and a fence is required as soon as the pool can hold water.

Source: Residential Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs (Maple Grove Building Department)

Lakeville / Dakota County

Building Inspections, City of LakevilleOnline portal: BS&A Online

  • Lakeville requires a swimming pool permit for pools with a depth of 24 inches at any point and a surface area over 150 square feet.
  • The submittal checklist asks for a certificate of survey showing lot dimensions, existing structures, the pool location and setback, the fence and equipment locations, and self-closing gates.
  • The fence needs a separate zoning permit, a heater needs a separate mechanical permit and a deck needs a separate building permit.
  • Pools must be at least 10 feet from adjoining lots and other structures and cannot be in a front yard. The city lists pool footing and final inspections, and the fence must pass inspection before filling.

Source: Swimming Pools Zoning Requirements (Lakeville, revised 5/21/2024)

Minneapolis / Hennepin County

Minneapolis Development Review

  • Minneapolis lists only prefabricated swimming pools that meet its size guidelines among construction work exempt from a permit.
  • The city's permit pages direct applicants to review the code and confirm with the city that a project does not need a permit.

Source: Work Exempt from Permit (City of Minneapolis) · Construction permits overview (City of Minneapolis)

Minnesota state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
Minnesota Residential Code, Minn. R. ch. 1309 (2018 IRC with amendments, Department of Labor and Industry), applies statewide and is enforced by municipalities that administer the State Building Code; 2024 IRC rulemaking began February 2026.
Pool barriers
Set by local ordinance; Minn. R. 1309.0326 deletes IRC Section R326 and the state deletes all IRC appendices except K and Q.
Contractor licensing
Department of Labor and Industry residential building contractor license when the work involves two or more special skills; pools, spas and hot tubs are a listed special skill (Minn. Stat. 326B.802, subd. 15).
Public pools
Minnesota Department of Health, Minn. R. ch. 4717 public swimming pool parts 4717.0150 to 4717.3975, with plan submission under part 4717.0450.
Engineer licensing
Minnesota Board of Architecture, Engineering, Land Surveying, Landscape Architecture, Geoscience and Interior Design

MINNESOTA QUESTIONS

Questions about Minnesota pool plans.

Does the Minnesota Residential Code set residential pool barrier rules?

No. Minnesota Rules 1309.0326 deletes IRC Section R326, and the state deletes IRC appendices except K and Q. Barrier and fence rules for residential pools come from each city's ordinance.

Who reviews public pool plans in Minnesota?

The Minnesota Department of Health. Plans and specifications are submitted under Minnesota Rules part 4717.0450, part of the public pool rules in chapter 4717, in addition to the local building permit.

Are your Minnesota pool plans sealed by a Minnesota-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Minnesota reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Minnesota jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Minnesota pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Minnesota?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
