MISSOURI · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Missouri.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

St. Charles County (unincorporated)

Community Development, Building and Code Enforcement DivisionOnline portal: Citizenserve Citizen Access Portal

  • St. Charles County's Residential Building Permit covers swimming pools, hot tubs and pool barriers on residentially zoned property, applied for in the Citizenserve portal.
  • Applicants submit the contractor names, building plans (PDF preferred) and a detailed plot plan showing the structure on the lot with distances to property lines.
  • The county says structures may not be located over easements or septic systems, and at least one inspection is required.
  • The county separately licenses pool installers through the Citizenserve portal, with a bond and liability insurance certificate.

Source: Residential Building Permit (St. Charles County) · Pool Installers License Application/Renewal (St. Charles County) · Community Development (St. Charles County)

Springfield / Greene County

Building Development Services

  • Springfield's swimming pool page covers outdoor private pools, including in-ground pools, hot tubs and spas, and says the pool barrier rules come from the International Building Code.
  • The city lists a barrier at least 48 inches above finished ground level, with no opening that passes a 4 inch sphere.
  • Pedestrian gates must open outward away from the pool and be self-closing and self-latching.
  • Springfield requires an approved building permit before any new one- or two-family house or accessory building is built, and the plans must include a site plan drawn to scale.

Source: Swimming Pools (City of Springfield) · 1 & 2 Family Permits (City of Springfield)

Wentzville / St. Charles County

Community Development, Building DivisionOnline portal: SmartGov Permit Portal

  • Wentzville lists a Swimming Pool/Hot Tub Permit among the work that needs a building permit.
  • Since 23 February 2026 all building permit applications go through the SmartGov Permit Portal, and the city no longer accepts paper plan submittals.
  • The city says it has adopted the 2021 IBC, 2021 IRC and 2021 IPC, plus the 2020 National Electrical Code, with local amendments.
  • Contractors, general and sub, must hold a city-issued contractor's license.

Source: Building Permits (City of Wentzville) · SmartGov Permit Portal (City of Wentzville) · Building Codes (City of Wentzville)

Lake Saint Louis / St. Charles County

Building Division, City of Lake Saint LouisOnline portal: Citizenserve Online Portal

  • Lake Saint Louis takes building permit applications and plans through its Citizenserve portal and publishes a pool requirement checklist.
  • The checklist asks for a site plan at 1 inch = 20 feet prepared by a licensed engineer or surveyor, with easements, elevations, proposed pool rim elevation and drainage contours.
  • The checklist also asks for the proposed pool barrier details, self-closing outward-swinging gates, door alarms, an erosion control plan, and equipment location with screening.
  • Contractors must hold a City Business License before doing construction work in the city.

Source: Building Permits (City of Lake Saint Louis) · Swimming Pool Requirement Checklist (City of Lake Saint Louis)

Missouri state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
No statewide residential code for private construction; the state adopts codes only for state-owned buildings, and cities and counties adopt their own.
Pool barriers
Set by local ordinance or the locally adopted code; no state pool barrier statute.
Contractor licensing
No statewide license for residential pool contractors; licensed or registered locally by cities and counties.
Public pools
Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, 19 CSR 20-3.020 Sanitation of Public Bathing Places, requiring written approval of plans and specifications before installation.
Engineer licensing
Missouri Board for Architects, Professional Engineers, Professional Land Surveyors and Professional Landscape Architects

MISSOURI QUESTIONS

Questions about Missouri pool plans.

Does Missouri have a statewide residential code for pool permits?

No. Missouri adopts codes only for state-owned buildings. Cities and counties adopt their own residential and pool codes, so code editions and barrier rules vary between jurisdictions such as St. Louis County and Kansas City.

Who approves public pool plans in Missouri?

Under 19 CSR 20-3.020, the owner submits plans and specifications to the state health department at least 30 days ahead and needs written approval before installation, plus final approval before opening. Local building permits also apply.

Are your Missouri pool plans sealed by a Missouri-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Missouri reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Missouri jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Missouri pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Missouri?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
