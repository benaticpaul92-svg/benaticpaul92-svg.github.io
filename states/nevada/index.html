NEVADA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Nevada.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Reno

City of Reno Development Services (Building and Safety)Online portal: ONE Regional Licensing and Permits (Accela Citizen Access)

  • Reno publishes a Residential Pool Submittal Guidelines sheet; plans must show barriers, fences and gates, including height, openings and latches.
  • The same sheet asks for pool equipment spec sheets, pipe and conduit installation depth, an equipotential bonding detail, and gas piping sizing calculations.
  • It also asks for electrical load calculations, circuits and GFCI protection, structural calculations for a cast-in-place pool, and manufacturer installation instructions for a fiberglass pool.
  • All building permit applications go in electronically through the ONE portal; since January 1, 2026 plans are accepted only under the 2024 edition of the building codes.

Source: Residential Pool Submittal Guidelines (updated 07/01/2025) - City of Reno · Building Permits - City of Reno Development Services · ONE Regional Licensing and Permits portal

Las Vegas

City of Las Vegas Building and SafetyOnline portal: Citizen Portal (Dashboard)

  • Permits that need plans are applied for through the Dashboard (Citizen Portal); permits without plans use the Online Building Permits page.
  • The city requires all new commercial, residential and civil building plans to be submitted electronically.
  • The city says it adopted the 2021 International Building Code and International Fire Code, effective March 23, 2023.
  • The building permits page does not mention pools, so pool-specific questions go to Customer Service.

Source: Building & Offsite Permits - City of Las Vegas

Clark County (unincorporated Las Vegas Valley)

Clark County Building & Fire PreventionOnline portal: Citizen Access Portal (Accela)

  • Building & Fire Prevention takes permit applications through the Citizen Access Portal.
  • The plan submittal section lists 'Do I Need a Permit?', how-to guides, application forms and a plan submittal FAQ.
  • An owner who intends to construct, enlarge, alter or repair a structure must first apply for a permit; exemptions are in Building Administrative Code section 22.02.190.

Source: Building Plan Submittal - Clark County · Building Permit Issuance - Clark County · Do I Need a Permit - Clark County · Clark County Citizen Access Portal

Nevada state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
No statewide residential code for private construction; counties and cities adopt the IRC locally, with Clark County on the 2024 IRC with Southern Nevada amendments from January 11, 2026.
Pool barriers
Set by locally adopted codes; Clark County adopted the 2024 ISPSC with Southern Nevada amendments (effective January 11, 2026), and other jurisdictions adopt their own ISPSC/IRC editions.
Contractor licensing
Nevada State Contractors Board, subclassification A-10 (Commercial and Residential Pools) or limited sub-subclassifications such as A-10a Residential Pools and A-10d Pools and Spas of Fiberglass (NAC 624.150).
Public pools
NAC Chapter 444 (public bathing places), with plans approved in writing by the health authority before construction under NRS 444.080 (Southern Nevada Health District in Clark County, otherwise the Division of Public and Behavioral Health or local health authority).
Engineer licensing
Nevada State Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors

NEVADA QUESTIONS

Questions about Nevada pool plans.

Which pool barrier code applies in Clark County?

Clark County adopted the 2024 ISPSC and 2024 IRC with Southern Nevada amendments, effective January 11, 2026. Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas adopt their own editions. We confirm the edition in force for your jurisdiction before drawings are issued.

Who approves public pool plans in Nevada?

Under NRS 444.080, plans go to the health authority before construction: the Southern Nevada Health District in Clark County, or the state Division of Public and Behavioral Health elsewhere. Structural adequacy must be certified by an architect or professional engineer.

Are your Nevada pool plans sealed by a Nevada-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Nevada reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Nevada jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Nevada pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Nevada?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
