NEW JERSEY · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for New Jersey.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Paramus (Bergen County)

Borough of Paramus Building Department

  • The borough's pool permit checklist lists a zoning review application, a site work application if there is a concrete walkway, patio or equipment pad, and a pool permit application covering small construction, building, electric, plumbing and soil.
  • The checklist asks for a survey showing pool, walkway, patio and filter locations with setbacks, plus spec sheets for heater, filter, pump, drains and fence and lock, a gas riser diagram, and an electrical diagram that must comply with 2020 NEC 680.
  • Borough handout: in-ground and above-ground pools must be 10 ft from rear and side yard setbacks and at least 5 ft from any house, shed or garage; filter equipment must be 10 ft from those lot lines.
  • Borough handout: an in-ground pool fence must be at least 4 ft high with self-closing, latching gates that swing outward; pools need building, electric and plumbing permits and a survey no more than 20 years old.

Source: Checklist for Pool Permit Submittals (revised 2/19/25) - Borough of Paramus · Requirements for Sheds, Fences, Pools, Hot Tub/Spa, Patios, Decks and Driveways - Borough of Paramus · Licenses & Permits - Borough of Paramus

Edison (Middlesex County)

Edison Township Code Enforcement (Construction Department)

  • The township says a permit is generally required to construct decks or pools, additions and fences.
  • Applications need two copies of plans, prepared by the homeowner for a single-family dwelling or by a New Jersey licensed architect or engineer.
  • Additions, fences, sheds and pools require a plot plan showing existing and proposed structures, distances to lot lines and any easements.
  • Process steps are zoning review, then building, electrical, plumbing and fire subcode review, then submission of prior approvals and permit issuance.

Source: Code Enforcement - Edison Township

Jersey City (Hudson County)

Jersey City Division of the Construction Code OfficialOnline portal: Online Permitting and Licensing System

  • Pools need zoning approval first; after that, pools over 24 inches need a permit application with the building and technical sections attached.
  • In-ground pools need a Bonding and Grounding Certificate and an annual city inspection.
  • Construction applications are made on the city's online permit portal; plans and plumbing and electrical technical forms must also be submitted in person at the Construction Code Official's Office.
  • Plans must carry the seal and signature of the registered architect or licensed engineer, with an exception for a homeowner preparing their own single-family plans under affidavit.

Source: Permit Specific FAQ - Jersey City Construction Code · Application & Review Process - Jersey City Construction Code · Online Permitting and Licensing - Jersey City · Construction Code - Jersey City

Wall Township (Monmouth County)

Wall Township Construction Department

  • The Construction Department posts the Uniform Construction Code Construction Permit Application with separate building, electrical, plumbing and fire protection subcode applications.

Source: Township Forms & Applications - Wall Township

Statewide

  • State Uniform Construction Code permit forms (F100 plus subcode technical sections) are submitted to the local enforcing office as one original plus three photocopies; project type decides which subcode sections apply. Source: Construction Permit Forms - NJ Department of Community Affairs

New Jersey state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23), mandatory statewide; the 2024 model codes including the 2024 IRC (NJ edition) took effect August 17, 2026, with a grace period for the 2021 editions.
Pool barriers
Uniform Construction Code: the one- and two-family dwelling subcode (IRC Section R326) requires pools and spas to comply with the ISPSC (2021 ISPSC under the 2021 NJ edition).
Contractor licensing
Home Improvement Contractor registration with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs; a separate pool and spa builder/installer license under N.J.S.A. 45:5A-39 et seq. (P.L. 2019, c. 22) has been enacted but is not yet issued.
Public pools
NJ Department of Health, N.J.A.C. 8:26 (Public Recreational Bathing): design follows the UCC, and plans sealed by a design professional need approval from local code officials and the health authority (N.J.A.C. 8:26-2.2).
Engineer licensing
New Jersey State Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors

NEW JERSEY QUESTIONS

Questions about New Jersey pool plans.

Which code governs residential pools in New Jersey?

The Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23) applies statewide. Its one- and two-family subcode sends pool and spa design to the ISPSC. The 2024 model codes took effect August 17, 2026, with a grace period for 2021 code designs.

Do public pools in New Jersey need health department approval?

Yes. Under N.J.A.C. 8:26-2.2, plans sealed by a design professional go to local code officials and the health authority at the same time. Construction cannot start until both have approved in writing.

Are your New Jersey pool plans sealed by a New Jersey-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in New Jersey reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your New Jersey jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare New Jersey pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in New Jersey?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
