New Jersey at a Glance

New Jersey pool permit requirements

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23), mandatory statewide; the 2024 model codes including the 2024 IRC (NJ edition) took effect August 17, 2026, with a grace period for the 2021 editions.
Pool barriers
Uniform Construction Code: the one- and two-family dwelling subcode (IRC Section R326) requires pools and spas to comply with the ISPSC (2021 ISPSC under the 2021 NJ edition).
Contractor licensing
Home Improvement Contractor registration with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs; a separate pool and spa builder/installer license under N.J.S.A. 45:5A-39 et seq. (P.L. 2019, c. 22) has been enacted but is not yet issued.
Public pools
NJ Department of Health, N.J.A.C. 8:26 (Public Recreational Bathing): design follows the UCC, and plans sealed by a design professional need approval from local code officials and the health authority (N.J.A.C. 8:26-2.2).
Engineer licensing
New Jersey State Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors
Questions

Frequently Asked

Which code governs residential pools in New Jersey?

The Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23) applies statewide. Its one- and two-family subcode sends pool and spa design to the ISPSC. The 2024 model codes took effect August 17, 2026, with a grace period for 2021 code designs.

Do public pools in New Jersey need health department approval?

Yes. Under N.J.A.C. 8:26-2.2, plans sealed by a design professional go to local code officials and the health authority at the same time. Construction cannot start until both have approved in writing.

Are your New Jersey pool plans sealed by a New Jersey-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in New Jersey reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your New Jersey jurisdiction.
