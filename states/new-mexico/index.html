NEW MEXICO · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for New Mexico.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Albuquerque

City of Albuquerque Planning Department, Building Safety DivisionOnline portal: ABQ-PLAN

  • The city's building safety FAQ lists building a swimming pool among the projects that need a permit.
  • Building permits are applied for, tracked and paid through the ABQ-PLAN online permitting portal.
  • The homeowner guide asks for a site plan showing the pool at least 5 ft from adjacent property lines and the dwelling, with dimensions that allow square footage and gallons to be checked.
  • The same guide (dated 2018) says the plan must show barrier compliance, with walls at least 4 ft high with an automatic cover or 6 ft without one, labeled gates, and UL 2017 alarms on dwelling doors opening to the pool.
  • The same guide (dated 2018) asks for a current architect or engineer seal on gunite pools, or an ICC-ES report for fiberglass pools.

Source: Building Safety & Permits FAQs - City of Albuquerque · Permits - City of Albuquerque · ABQ-PLAN - City of Albuquerque · Home Owner's Building Permit Guide - City of Albuquerque

Rio Rancho

City of Rio Rancho Development ServicesOnline portal: Click2Gov

  • The city lists swimming pools, hot tubs and spas among the projects that need a permit.
  • The pool permit packet asks for a site plan, a building permit and plan review application, plumbing or mechanical permits, an electrical permit, and a floor plan showing plumbing, mechanical and electrical layouts.
  • Applications go in through the Click2Gov portal or by email to permits@rrnm.gov; the form says paper documents are not accepted.
  • The packet reproduces the city fence rule: a pool deeper than 3 ft or over 5,000 gallons needs a barrier of at least 6 ft, with self-closing gates.

Source: Swimming Pool Permit Packet - City of Rio Rancho · Get a Permit - City of Rio Rancho

City of Santa Fe

City of Santa Fe Land Use Department, Building DivisionOnline portal: Citizen Self-Service (CSS) portal

  • The Building Permits page lists New Residential permits, including pools, as a permit type with its own submittal instructions.
  • Trade and secondary permits are applied for online through the Citizen Self-Service portal; the Building Division counter is by appointment.
  • The city lists the 2021 NM Residential Building Code and the 2012 NM Swimming Pool, Spa, and Hot Tub Code among its adopted codes.

Source: Building Permits - City of Santa Fe · Adopted Construction Codes - City of Santa Fe

Santa Fe County (unincorporated)

Santa Fe County Growth Management, Building and Development Services

  • The county publishes development permit packets for residential projects, available online or at its office at 240 Grant Avenue.
  • County Ordinance 2007-1 says a person building a swimming pool must apply to the Water Resources Department and the Land Use Department, describing the water supply.
  • That ordinance also requires an automatic cover on each outdoor pool and a fire-marshal-approved draft hydrant for each new pool.

Source: Residential Development - Santa Fe County · Ordinance 2007-1 - Santa Fe County

New Mexico state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
2021 New Mexico Residential Building Code (14.7.3 NMAC, 2021 IRC with state amendments), adopted by the Construction Industries Division, effective July 14, 2023.
Pool barriers
14.7.3.25 NMAC (Chapter 45, pools and spas on one- and two-family dwelling lots), which replaces IRC Section R326 and requires a barrier at least 48 inches high.
Contractor licensing
Construction Industries Division (Regulation and Licensing Department), classification GS-25 Swimming Pools; pool plumbing falls under MM-1 Plumbing.
Public pools
New Mexico Environment Department, 7.18.2 NMAC (Public Swimming Pools, Spas and Baths: General Provisions), requiring a construction permit with plans stamped by a New Mexico registered engineer or architect.
Engineer licensing
New Mexico State Board of Licensure for Professional Engineers and Professional Surveyors

NEW MEXICO QUESTIONS

Questions about New Mexico pool plans.

Where are New Mexico's residential pool barrier rules?

In the 2021 New Mexico Residential Building Code, 14.7.3 NMAC. It deletes IRC Section R326 and adds Chapter 45 (14.7.3.25 NMAC) for pools on one- and two-family lots, with a 48 inch minimum barrier height.

Do public pool plans in New Mexico need an engineer's stamp?

Yes. 7.18.2 NMAC requires a New Mexico Environment Department construction permit before a public pool is built or modified, with plans stamped and signed by a New Mexico registered professional engineer or architect.

Are your New Mexico pool plans sealed by a New Mexico-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in New Mexico reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your New Mexico jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare New Mexico pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in New Mexico?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
