NEW YORK · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for New York.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

New York City

NYC Department of BuildingsOnline portal: DOB NOW

  • The Department of Buildings requires work permits to install a pool, with limited exceptions such as some above-ground and shallow wading pools.
  • Pool construction requirements are in section 3109 of the NYC Building Code.
  • A permit is not required for an outdoor pool at a one- or two-family home that is limited to 400 sq ft and set back farther than its deepest depth, subject to a plumbing condition.
  • If the work goes beyond the exempt items, a Registered Design Professional must submit construction plans to the Department for approval before the permit is issued.
  • NYC311 lists a 4 ft fence with a self-closing gate around in-ground pools, and 5 ft from the lot line on lots 25 ft wide or more.

Source: Decks, Porches & Swimming Pools - NYC Department of Buildings · Project Requirements for Owner - Outdoor Swimming Pool - NYC Department of Buildings · Swimming Pool Installation Rules - NYC311

Town of Huntington (Suffolk County)

Town of Huntington Building & Housing Department

  • A building permit is required for a swimming pool in the unincorporated Town; the villages of Asharoken, Huntington Bay, Lloyd Harbor and Northport handle their own permits.
  • The filing list includes two notarized pool permit applications, two surveys, two plot plans showing fence and setbacks, and two sets of pool plans showing size, depth and construction type.
  • The pool and its equipment must be at least 5 ft from the property line, and a fence enclosure with gates is required under Appendix G of the state code.
  • Electrical work must follow NEC Article 680, and an electrical inspection certificate is needed before a Certificate of Completion.

Source: Requirements for Swimming Pool Permit - Town of Huntington

Town of Oyster Bay (Nassau County)

Town of Oyster Bay Department of Planning and Development, Building DivisionOnline portal: Building Division Portal (eTRAKiT)

  • The Building Division lists in-ground and above-ground pools 18 in. deep or more among projects needing a building permit.
  • The Town code requires a permit application with plans showing width, length and depth, plus a survey showing the pool location, for pools over 5,000 gallons.
  • The Town code sets pools at least 5 ft from any lot line, with a fence of 4 to 6 ft and self-closing, self-latching gates.
  • Pools with only required fencing, and related pool heaters, are listed as portal permit types.

Source: Building Division - Town of Oyster Bay · Building Portal - Town of Oyster Bay · Town of Oyster Bay Code Chapter 246 (swimming pools)

Town of Greenburgh (Westchester County)

Town of Greenburgh Department of Buildings

  • The building permit instructions list extra submittals for pools and hot tubs: approved slope and wetlands clearance forms, pool specifications, and a site plan with distances to property lines.
  • A zoning compliance form completed by a NYS licensed architect or engineer is required for any new pool or deck.
  • If decking is proposed around the pool, a NYS licensed architect or engineer must prepare the drawings, and details of NYS pool barrier compliance must be provided.
  • Septic or well systems need Westchester County Health Department approval with the application.

Source: Building Permit Application and Instructions - Town of Greenburgh

Town of Amherst (Erie County)

Town of Amherst Building DepartmentOnline portal: Amherst Permits, Inspections & Inquiries

  • The Town recommends applying through its online permit portal, which requires a registered user account.
  • In-ground pool applications need a survey, stamped drawings by a design professional, a signed Pool Regulations Letter, a National Grid pool clearance letter, a pool alarm brochure and an electrical subcontractor statement.
  • The check sheet says in-ground pool plans must bear a NYS professional engineer or architect seal if capacity exceeds 2,000 cu ft, and must include grade and drainage plans and soils test information.
  • Plumbing needs a separate permit, and electrical and plumbing work must be done by Town of Amherst licensed contractors.

Source: Pool Permits - Town of Amherst Building Department · Building Permit Application Check Sheet: Swimming Pools & Hot Tubs - Town of Amherst

Town of Greece (Monroe County)

Town of Greece Technical Services Department / Building Department

  • Any new swimming pool or spa deeper than 24 in. needs a permit.
  • The submittal needs a site plan or current survey with the pool location and setbacks to property lines and structures.
  • Pool enclosures must follow Appendix G of the Residential Code of New York State.
  • Electrical work is inspected by one of three listed inspection agencies, and the Building Department does the final inspection.

Source: Swimming Pools/Spas & Pool Enclosures - Town of Greece

New York state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
2025 Residential Code of New York State (part of the Uniform Code, based on the 2024 IRC), mandatory statewide outside New York City for permit applications filed on or after December 31, 2025.
Pool barriers
Residential Code of New York State swimming pool provisions (barrier at least 48 inches high, plus an ASTM F2208 pool alarm for pools installed or substantially modified after December 14, 2006); New York City applies its own code.
Contractor licensing
No statewide license for residential pool contractors; licensed locally, for example by NYC DCWP and by counties such as Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester.
Public pools
NYS Department of Health, State Sanitary Code 10 NYCRR Subpart 6-1 (Swimming Pools): plans prepared by a New York licensed engineer or architect must be approved by the permit-issuing official, with design standards in Section 6-1.29.
Engineer licensing
New York State Board for Engineering, Land Surveying and Geology (NYSED Office of the Professions)

NEW YORK QUESTIONS

Questions about New York pool plans.

Is a pool alarm required in New York?

Yes. Residential pools installed, built or substantially modified after December 14, 2006 need a pool alarm classified to ASTM F2208, in addition to a compliant barrier at least 48 inches high under the Uniform Code.

Does New York City follow the state Residential Code?

No. The Residential Code of New York State applies statewide except New York City, which enforces its own NYC Construction Codes. Elsewhere, the 2025 Uniform Code applies to permit applications filed on or after December 31, 2025.

Are your New York pool plans sealed by a New York-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in New York reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your New York jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare New York pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in New York?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
