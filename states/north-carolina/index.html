NORTH CAROLINA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for North Carolina.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Raleigh

City of Raleigh Planning and Development (Development Services)Online portal: Permit and Development Portal

  • Building and electrical permits are required for any pool, spa or hot tub that can hold more than 24 in. of water.
  • A plumbing permit and backflow device are also needed if the pool connects to the home's water supply.
  • Applicants submit a survey of existing conditions and the proposed work, the fence (barrier) elevation, and an Owner Exemption Affidavit if the owner acts as contractor, all as PDFs through the Permit and Development Portal.
  • Pools, decks, equipment and fences may not sit in a public sewer easement or a public or private drainage easement, and pools and decks may not be built over septic systems or their repair areas.
  • The city's pool guide gives a barrier at least 48 in. above grade, self-closing and self-latching gates, and door alarms or a powered cover where the house wall is part of the barrier.

Source: Installing a Pool or Spa - City of Raleigh · ABCs of Installing a Pool or Spa - City of Raleigh

New Hanover County (Wilmington area)

New Hanover County Building Safety and Planning & Land Use (Zoning)

  • The county's residential permit chart lists a pool as needing building and electrical permits, with its permit application links on the chart.
  • Pools under 600 sq ft may sit as close as 5 ft to side and rear property lines.
  • Pools of 600 sq ft and larger must meet setbacks based on the property's zoning classification.
  • The setback is measured to the pool depression of an in-ground pool, not to the surrounding concrete slab.

Source: Residential Permits chart - New Hanover County · FAQ: Do I need a permit for a swimming pool? - New Hanover County

Concord (Cabarrus County)

City of Concord Planning & Development ServicesOnline portal: Accela Citizen Access

  • The permits page groups pools with accessory structures, sheds, decks and covered porches under one residential application.
  • Applicants fill in and digitally sign the application, save it as a PDF, and upload it to the Accela application.
  • All documents uploaded in the Accela system must be in PDF format.

Source: Permits - City of Concord Planning & Development Services

Asheville (Buncombe County)

City of Asheville Development Services Department, Permit Application CenterOnline portal: Development Portal

  • The city's residential permits page lists swimming pools under the guide for building a detached structure or deck.
  • Residential applications for these structures can be submitted online through the Development Portal or in person at the Permit Application Center at 161 South Charlotte Street.

Source: Residential property and permits - City of Asheville · Build a detached structure or deck - City of Asheville

North Carolina state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
2018 North Carolina Residential Code, mandatory statewide; the 2024 edition is published but its effective date was deferred by S.L. 2025-2, though an owner may request the 2024 code.
Pool barriers
North Carolina Residential Code appendix for pools: Appendix AV (Section AV105 barriers) in the 2018 NCRC, moved to Appendix NC-A (Section NCA105) in the 2024 NCRC.
Contractor licensing
North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors, Swimming Pool specialty classification (Limited, Intermediate or Unlimited), required for projects of $40,000 or more.
Public pools
NC Department of Health and Human Services, 15A NCAC 18A .2500 (Public Swimming Pools); plans are submitted through the local health department for Department approval under .2509.
Engineer licensing
North Carolina Board of Examiners for Engineers and Surveyors

NORTH CAROLINA QUESTIONS

Questions about North Carolina pool plans.

Which NC Residential Code edition applies to pool permits?

The 2018 NC Residential Code remains in force. S.L. 2025-2 delayed the 2024 code until 12 months after the State Fire Marshal certifies set conditions, though an owner may request the 2024 code. Barrier rules sit in Appendix AV.

When does a North Carolina pool builder need a state license?

For projects of $40,000 or more, the builder needs a license from the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors, such as the Swimming Pool specialty classification, at the Limited, Intermediate or Unlimited level.

Are your North Carolina pool plans sealed by a North Carolina-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in North Carolina reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your North Carolina jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare North Carolina pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in North Carolina?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
