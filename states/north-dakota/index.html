NORTH DAKOTA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Pool permit notes for Fargo, West Fargo, Bismarck and more in North Dakota.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on November 15, 2026 unless a card gives a later date. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your request.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the quote form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Jump to: Fargo West Fargo Bismarck Mandan Williston

Fargo pool permit notes

City of Fargo Inspections Department

Online portal: Fargo online permit portal

  • Residential permit applications are submitted through the city's online permit portal, where plan reviewers send status updates by email.
  • The city's FAQ says private pools holding more than 24 in. of water must be surrounded by a fence or barrier at least 48 in. tall, set at least 4 ft from the outside of the pool.
2 more permit notes
  • Gates in the pool barrier must be self-closing and self-latching, and the FAQ cites City of Fargo Ordinance No 5425.
  • The city lists local amendments to the 2024 International Building Code on its code requirements page.

Source: Inspections FAQs - City of Fargo · Residential Permit & Fees - City of Fargo · Residential Permit Application - City of Fargo · Code Requirements - City of Fargo

West Fargo pool permit notes

City of West Fargo Building Inspections

Online portal: West Fargo online permit portal (iWorQ)

  • A building permit is required before building or placing a pool, spa or hot tub with water deeper than 2 ft, and an inspection is required when the installation is finished.
  • The pool guideline says applicants apply in the online portal using the Residential - New Accessory Structure / Shed sub type and upload a digital site plan.
2 more permit notes
  • The site plan must show lot lines and easements, existing buildings, pool dimensions and distances to property lines, fence and gate locations, and any new paving.
  • The city adopts a local amendment of International Property Maintenance Code section 303 for pool barriers, with the top at least 48 in. above grade for pools deeper than 24 in.

Source: Guideline for Installing Swimming Pools - City of West Fargo · Residential Building Permits - City of West Fargo · Building Inspections - City of West Fargo

Bismarck pool permit notes

City of Bismarck Community Development Department, Building Inspections Division
  • The city's Building Inspections Division, not Environmental Health, handles barrier protection for residential swimming pools.
  • Bismarck lists pools among the projects that need a permit, and it publishes a Residential Inground Swimming Pool permit form.
2 more permit notes
  • The form asks the applicant to choose a barrier type, a 6-foot fence or a powered safety cover, and to give the pool dimensions and its distances to the side and rear property lines.
  • Required documents listed on the form are the pool installation manual and an overview of the property showing where the pool will sit; a powered cover also needs proof it meets ASTM F 1346-91.

Source: Swimming Pool Establishments - City of Bismarck · Permits Are Required For - City of Bismarck · Residential Inground Swimming Pool permit form (Building Inspections Division, rev. 4/21/2022) · Checked November 18, 2026

Mandan pool permit notes

City of Mandan Building Inspections
  • Mandan's own permit guide and FAQ list swimming pools among the work that does not need a city building permit.
  • The same lists say fences up to 8.5 feet high do not need a permit.
2 more permit notes
  • The city says it adopts the current North Dakota State Building Code, with its own amendments for the city and its extraterritorial area.
  • The guide reminds owners that North Dakota Century Code 49-23 requires calling 811 before any excavation.

Source: Building Guide - City of Mandan Building Department · Frequently Asked Questions - Mandan, ND · Checked November 18, 2026

Williston pool permit notes

City of Williston Building Safety / Development Services
  • As of January 1, 2026, Williston follows the North Dakota State Building Code, which includes the 2024 International Building Code, 2024 International Residential Code and 2018 Uniform Plumbing Code.
  • The city lists NFPA 70 2026 edition (National Electrical Code) among the codes it follows.
2 more permit notes
  • Applications need the property address and legal description, the estimated construction cost, and a site plan showing existing buildings and the proposed project.
  • Applications and plans are sent to the building safety office by email, and incomplete applications are rejected. The city's pages do not have a pool-specific checklist.

Source: Building Safety Frequently Asked Questions - City of Williston · Building Permit Applications - City of Williston · Checked November 18, 2026

North Dakota state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so name your permit office in your request.

Residential code
2024 IRC as part of the North Dakota State Building Code from January 1, 2026 (NDCC 54-21.3); local enforcement is optional, but cities, counties and townships that enforce codes must use the state code.
Pool barriers
Set by local ordinance in jurisdictions that enforce codes; the State Building Code list (IBC, IRC, IMC, IFGC) does not include the ISPSC.
Contractor licensing
North Dakota Secretary of State contractor license (Class A to D by job size) under NDCC chapter 43-07, required when a job exceeds $4,000; no pool-specific class.
Engineer licensing
North Dakota State Board of Registration for Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors

NORTH DAKOTA QUESTIONS

Questions about North Dakota pool plans.

Is the North Dakota State Building Code mandatory?

Only where adopted. Cities, counties and townships may choose to enforce building codes, and if they do they must use the State Building Code, the 2024 IRC from January 1, 2026, with local amendments allowed.

Does a pool builder need a North Dakota license?

Yes, when a job exceeds $4,000. Contractors are licensed by the North Dakota Secretary of State under NDCC chapter 43-07, in Class A to D according to contract amount per job.

Can my North Dakota pool plans be sealed by an engineer licensed in North Dakota?

Only when a Professional Engineer licensed in North Dakota takes your project and directs the engineering while the drawings are prepared. JEBN prepares the drawings and does not seal them.

What if plan review comes back with comments?

Send us the comments. Your quote states the revision support included, and we identify the sheets each comment affects. Comments on the engineering go to the engineer of record.

How fast can you prepare North Dakota pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. Where your permit needs an engineer’s seal, the engineer’s work takes additional time, and so does the permit office’s own review.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in North Dakota?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings that respond to it.
