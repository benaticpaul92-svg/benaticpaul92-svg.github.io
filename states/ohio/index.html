OHIO · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Ohio.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Lakewood (Cuyahoga County)

City of Lakewood Department of Housing and BuildingOnline portal: CitizenServe

  • The city's permit table marks swimming pools, hot tubs and saunas as needing a permit, construction drawings and zoning review.
  • The planning guide says plans must be approved and a construction permit issued before work begins, and the application needs a site plan showing the pool, equipment, fences, gates, receptacles and buildings.
  • The guide cites Chapter 1722 (Family Swimming Pools) of the Lakewood Codified Ordinances, with a fence at least 4 ft high and a self-closing, self-latching gate.
  • The guide asks for pool and circulation capacity, filtration and sanitizer type, and backflow protection under Ohio Plumbing Code Section 608.13.
  • The guide's site plan notes set pool distance to rear and side property lines by zoning district, such as 10 ft in R1 districts, and bonding with a solid No. 8 AWG or larger copper conductor.

Source: Building Permits - City of Lakewood · Permit Requirements - City of Lakewood · Swimming Pool and Hot Tub Planning Guide - City of Lakewood

Dublin (Franklin County)

City of Dublin Building Standards and Review ServicesOnline portal: Accela Citizen Access

  • The city lists swimming pools and hot tubs among residential projects that need a building permit.
  • All planning and permit applications are processed online through the Accela Citizen Access portal, with drawings submitted through ProjectDox electronic plan review.
  • The city says to ask Planning whether a zoning permit is also required for a project.

Source: Building Permits & Applications - City of Dublin

Cleveland (Cuyahoga County)

City of Cleveland Department of Building & HousingOnline portal: Permit Portal (Accela)

  • Residential permits are searched, applied for and paid through the city's Permit Portal.
  • Electrical, plumbing and HVAC contractors must first hold a State of Ohio license and then complete the city's contractor packet.

Source: Building & Housing - City of Cleveland

Ohio state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
2019 Residential Code of Ohio (OAC Chapter 4101:8, based on the 2018 IRC), adopted by the Ohio Board of Building Standards and enforced by certified local residential building departments.
Pool barriers
Set by local ordinance; Ohio cities and townships adopt their own residential pool barrier rules, typically the IRC or ISPSC barrier provisions.
Contractor licensing
No statewide license for residential pool contractors; the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board licenses only electrical, plumbing, hydronics, HVAC and refrigeration contractors, and pool builders register locally.
Public pools
Ohio Department of Health, OAC Chapter 3701-31 (public swimming pools, spas and special use pools); plans must be approved by the director of health before construction or substantial alteration (OAC 3701-31-05).
Engineer licensing
Ohio State Board of Registration for Professional Engineers and Surveyors

OHIO QUESTIONS

Questions about Ohio pool plans.

Who reviews public pool plans in Ohio?

The director of health. Under OAC 3701-31-05, no public swimming pool may be built, installed or substantially altered until the Ohio Department of Health approves the plans, submitted with an application signed by the pool designer.

Does Ohio license residential pool contractors?

No. The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board licenses only electrical, plumbing, hydronics, HVAC and refrigeration contractors. Pool builders may need local contractor registration, and residential pool barrier rules are set by city or township ordinance.

Are your Ohio pool plans sealed by a Ohio-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Ohio reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Ohio jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Ohio pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Ohio?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
