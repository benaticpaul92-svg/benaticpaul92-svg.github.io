OREGON · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Oregon.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Portland

Portland Permitting & DevelopmentOnline portal: Development Hub PDX

  • The city's pool page says a building permit is needed before installing a swimming pool, defined as a structure holding water over 24 inches deep, unless the pool is prefabricated with walls entirely above adjacent grade.
  • An electrical permit is required for in-ground pools, and the permit application forms listed are the building, electrical and mechanical permit applications.
  • The page calls for a barrier at least 4 ft high, a maximum 2 inch gap under it, openings that do not pass a 4 inch sphere, and a self-closing, latching gate that opens outward.
  • The pool must be at least 3 feet from any lot line, set back from adjacent slopes, and may face extra zoning limits in an overlay zone or plan district.

Source: Hot Tubs, Ponds, Swimming Pools, and Water Features (Portland.gov)

Medford

City of Medford Building Safety Department

  • The pool submittal guidelines ask for a building permit application, a Pool Barrier Acknowledgement form, and a to-scale site plan showing utility easements, setbacks and mechanical equipment location.
  • Site and pool construction documents must be at least 11x17, while engineering calculations and geotechnical reports may be 8.5x11.
  • The plans must state pool type (in-ground or above ground), depth, sand or cartridge filter, whether heated, and the auto fill and backflow device.
  • The final inspection checks a barrier at least 48 inches high, openings that stop a 4 inch sphere, and outward-opening self-latching, self-closing gates.
  • Medford requires a 4-foot high temporary or permanent fence around swimming pool excavations.

Source: Swimming Pool Submittal Guidelines and Swimming Pool Safety (City of Medford)

Gresham

City of Gresham Building Services

  • Gresham's list of work not needing a permit exempts prefabricated pools in R-3 occupancies where the pool walls are entirely above the adjacent grade.
  • The same list says the barrier requirements in Appendix G are not exempt, so barriers around swimming pools still need a permit.
  • The list excludes pool barriers from its exemption for fences not over 7 feet high.

Source: Projects That Do Not Require Permits (City of Gresham)

Statewide

  • Oregon Building Codes Division says a fence serving as a barrier around a swimming pool, hot tub or spa needs a residential building permit, and barriers around prefabricated above-grade pools are not exempt. Source: About Oregon residential building permits (Building Codes Division)

Oregon state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
Oregon Residential Specialty Code, mandatory statewide (Building Codes Division, OAR 918-480-0005): the 2026 ORSC based on the 2024 IRC took effect October 1, 2026 and is mandatory April 1, 2027; the 2023 ORSC (2021 IRC) may be used until then.
Pool barriers
Regulated statewide by the ORSC: pool barrier fences need a building permit everywhere in Oregon, and the 2026 ORSC Oregon amendment requires residential pool barriers to comply with ISPSC Section 305.
Contractor licensing
Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB) license required for anyone building pools for pay; residential pool builders typically hold a Residential Specialty Contractor endorsement.
Public pools
Oregon Health Authority, Public Pool and Tourist Facility Program: OAR 333-060-1000 (design standards and construction) and OAR 333-062-1000 (operation), which replaced the old divisions 060 and 062 in April 2025.
Engineer licensing
Oregon State Board of Examiners for Engineering and Land Surveying

OREGON QUESTIONS

Questions about Oregon pool plans.

Does an in-ground residential pool need a building permit in Oregon?

Under the 2023 ORSC, in-ground pools accessory to one- and two-family dwellings are exempt from building permits unless a municipal ordinance requires one. Pool barrier fences need a permit statewide, so barrier details belong on every submittal.

Which rules govern commercial pool design in Oregon?

Public pools and spas are designed to Oregon Health Authority rule OAR 333-060-1000, which replaced the old division 060 rules in April 2025. Plans go through pool plan review before construction, with some county health departments handling applications.

Are your Oregon pool plans sealed by a Oregon-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Oregon reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Oregon jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Oregon pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Oregon?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
