VERMONT · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Pool permit notes for Burlington, South Burlington, Rutland City and more in Vermont.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on November 15, 2026 unless a card gives a later date. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your request.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the quote form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Jump to: Burlington South Burlington Rutland City Stowe Essex Junction

Burlington pool permit notes

Burlington Department of Permitting & Inspections (Zoning Division and Building & Trades Division)

Online portal: OpenGov

  • Under Sec. 3.1.2(a) on exterior work, a zoning permit is required to install or remove a swimming pool.
  • Burlington says most projects that need a zoning permit also need building, electrical, plumbing or mechanical permits, and that zoning approval comes first.
2 more permit notes
  • Zoning, building and trade permits can be applied for together in the city’s online OpenGov permit system.
  • The Trades Division, at 802-863-9094, is the contact for what construction permits a project needs.

Source: Burlington Department of Permitting & Inspections · When Do I Need to Apply for a Zoning Permit? · Construction Permits

South Burlington pool permit notes

South Burlington Planning & Zoning (zoning); Fire Marshal and Electrical Inspection (building and electrical code inspections)

Permitting & Registration Portal

  • South Burlington says Planning and Zoning review is required for all land development projects.
  • City permit forms and applications can be submitted through the online Permitting & Registration Portal.
2 more permit notes
  • The Fire Marshal and Electrical Inspection service handles fire, building and electrical code compliance inspections.
  • Issued decisions, site plans and permits from earlier projects are archived online and can be searched by address.

Source: City Permits, Licenses, and Registrations

Rutland City pool permit notes

Rutland City Planning & Zoning Department and Building Department

Online portal: GovPilot eForms (Building & Zoning / DPW Permits)

  • Rutland’s Land Development Regulations list a swimming pool within the definition of a structure.
  • Zoning permit applications require an uploaded attachment such as a site plan or building plans, or the application is treated as incomplete.
2 more permit notes
  • Most zoning permits also need a separate building permit, which the Building Department handles.
  • The Building Department says both State and City building permits are required for most renovation projects in the city.

Source: Rutland City home page · Planning & Zoning Permit Applications · Building Department · Land Development Regulations (adopted 9-21-26)

Stowe pool permit notes

Town of Stowe Planning & Zoning
  • Stowe Planning & Zoning reviews and issues zoning permits under the Stowe Zoning and Subdivision Regulations.
  • An application is a Development Application form plus the checklist for the project type, emailed to pandz@stowevt.gov or dropped off at the office.
2 more permit notes
  • The zoning regulations define a structure to include swimming pools greater than 100 sq. ft.
  • In the Ridgelines/Hillsides Overlay District, Section 9.4(2) exempts ponds and swimming pools that require no site clearing from the overlay review.

Source: Stowe Planning & Zoning · Apply for a Zoning Permit · Stowe Zoning Regulations 2024

Essex Junction (Chittenden County) pool permit notes

City of Essex Junction Community Development Department
  • The city’s development fee schedule lists swimming pools as an accessory structure for single-family detached homes under Zoning/Building Permit fees, and Section 706.B.6 of the Land Development Code says all accessory uses or structures must obtain permits as specified in the Code unless specifically exempted.
  • Section 706.L says all in-ground pools must be completely enclosed by a wall, fence or other structure not less than four (4) feet in height, with entry through a self-latching and self-closing gate, although a house door that forms part of the enclosure does not need to be so equipped. Hedges or natural barriers may be allowed if they are dense enough to bar entry to the pool.
2 more permit notes
  • Section 706.L says no overhead electrical wires may cross the pool area or be installed within fifteen (15) feet of the pool surface. It also says above-ground pools need not meet the Section L.2 fencing requirement if evidence is submitted that the pool can be entered only by portable steps or stairs that are removed when the pool is not in use.
  • The city’s Zoning Permit Application asks for an attached site plan that clearly shows the location of the structure or use and its distance to all property lines.

Source: Land Development Code, adopted February 26, 2025 (City of Essex Junction) · Development Fee Schedule, effective February 12, 2026 (City of Essex Junction) · Zoning Permit Application (City of Essex Junction) · Community Development (City of Essex Junction) · Checked November 25, 2026

VERMONT REQUIREMENTS

What Vermont permit offices check.

Each city and county reviews pool plans against its own checklist. These are requirements and site conditions that can apply in Vermont. Structural design, including any wind, flood, seismic or uplift loads at your site, comes from your engineer or your builder’s standard details. We draw it.

  1. 01Pool barrier, gate and alarm details to the Vermont requirements that apply to your site
  2. 02Site plans and setbacks prepared for Burlington, South Burlington and Rutland submittals
  3. 03Deep frost penetration (about 5 ft) and severe freeze-thaw cycling
  4. 04High town-specific ground snow loads on enclosures and decks
  5. 05Shallow ledge and glacial till with perched groundwater and uplift
  6. 06Pump head (TDH) calculation sheet where the office asks for one, equipment pad and bonding details

JEBN does not seal drawings. If your permit office requires an engineer’s seal, a Professional Engineer licensed in Vermont must direct the engineering and seal the set.

Vermont state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so name your permit office in your request.

Residential code
No statewide building code for owner-occupied single-family homes. The Vermont Fire and Building Safety Code (Division of Fire Safety, 2021 IBC basis) covers public buildings only, and the Residential Building Energy Standards apply statewide.
Pool barriers
No state statute or adopted model code for residential pool barriers. Set by local (town) ordinance where one exists.
Contractor licensing
No pool-specific license. Contractors taking residential construction jobs of $10,000 or more must register with the Office of Professional Regulation (26 V.S.A. § 5501).
Engineer licensing
Vermont Board of Professional Engineering (Office of Professional Regulation)

VERMONT QUESTIONS

Questions about Vermont pool plans.

Does Vermont have a statewide building code for a backyard pool at a single-family home?

No. The Vermont Fire and Building Safety Code excludes owner-occupied single-family residences, so permits and barrier rules for a backyard pool come from the town’s zoning and local ordinances. Pools at public buildings fall under Division of Fire Safety jurisdiction.

Can my Vermont pool plans be sealed by an engineer licensed in Vermont?

Only when a Professional Engineer licensed in Vermont takes your project and directs the engineering while the drawings are prepared. JEBN prepares the drawings and does not seal them.

What if plan review comes back with comments?

Send us the comments. Your quote states the revisions included, and we identify the sheets each comment affects. Comments on the engineering go to the engineer of record.

How fast can you prepare Vermont pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. Where your permit needs an engineer’s seal, the engineer’s work takes additional time, and so does the permit office’s own review.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Vermont?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings that respond to it.
