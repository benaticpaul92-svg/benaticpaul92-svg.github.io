VERMONT · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Vermont.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Burlington

Burlington Department of Permitting & Inspections (Zoning Division and Building & Trades Division)Online portal: OpenGov

  • Under Sec. 3.1.2(a) on exterior work, a zoning permit is required to install or remove a swimming pool.
  • Burlington says most projects that need a zoning permit also need building, electrical, plumbing or mechanical permits, and that zoning approval comes first.
  • Zoning, building and trade permits can be applied for together in the city's online OpenGov permit system.
  • The Trades Division, at 802-863-9094, is the contact for what construction permits a project needs.

Source: Burlington Department of Permitting & Inspections · When Do I Need to Apply for a Zoning Permit? · Construction Permits

South Burlington

South Burlington Planning & Zoning (zoning); Fire Marshal and Electrical Inspection (building and electrical code inspections)Online portal: Permitting & Registration Portal

  • South Burlington says Planning and Zoning review is required for all land development projects.
  • City permit forms and applications can be submitted through the online Permitting & Registration Portal.
  • The Fire Marshal and Electrical Inspection service handles fire, building and electrical code compliance inspections.
  • Issued decisions, site plans and permits from earlier projects are archived online and can be searched by address.

Source: City Permits, Licenses, and Registrations

Rutland City

Rutland City Planning & Zoning Department and Building DepartmentOnline portal: GovPilot eForms (Building & Zoning / DPW Permits)

  • Rutland's Land Development Regulations list a swimming pool within the definition of a structure.
  • Zoning permit applications require an uploaded attachment such as a site plan or building plans, or the application is treated as incomplete.
  • Most zoning permits also need a separate building permit, which the Building Department handles.
  • The Building Department says both State and City building permits are required for most renovation projects in the city.

Source: Rutland City home page · Planning & Zoning Permit Applications · Building Department · Land Development Regulations (adopted 9-21-26)

Stowe

Town of Stowe Planning & Zoning

  • Stowe Planning & Zoning reviews and issues zoning permits under the Stowe Zoning and Subdivision Regulations.
  • An application is a Development Application form plus the checklist for the project type, emailed to pandz@stowevt.gov or dropped off at the office.
  • The zoning regulations define a structure to include swimming pools greater than 100 sq. ft.
  • In the Ridgelines/Hillsides Overlay District, Section 9.4(2) exempts ponds and swimming pools that require no site clearing from the overlay review.

Source: Stowe Planning & Zoning · Apply for a Zoning Permit · Stowe Zoning Regulations 2024

Vermont state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
No statewide building code for owner-occupied single-family homes; the Vermont Fire and Building Safety Code (Division of Fire Safety, 2021 IBC basis) covers public buildings only, and the Residential Building Energy Standards apply statewide.
Pool barriers
No state statute or adopted model code for residential pool barriers; set by local (town) ordinance where one exists.
Contractor licensing
No pool-specific license; contractors taking residential construction jobs of $10,000 or more must register with the Office of Professional Regulation (26 V.S.A. § 5501).
Engineer licensing
Vermont Board of Professional Engineering (Office of Professional Regulation)

VERMONT QUESTIONS

Questions about Vermont pool plans.

Does Vermont have a statewide building code for a backyard pool at a single-family home?

No. The Vermont Fire and Building Safety Code excludes owner-occupied single-family residences, so permits and barrier rules for a backyard pool come from the town's zoning and local ordinances. Pools at public buildings fall under Division of Fire Safety jurisdiction.

Are your Vermont pool plans sealed by a Vermont-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Vermont reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Vermont jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Vermont pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Vermont?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
