WEST VIRGINIA · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for West Virginia.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Berkeley County (Martinsburg area)

Berkeley County Building Permits and Inspections

  • The county's pool checklist requires an application, scaled drawings showing the pool, fence and gates and distances from property lines, and a manufacturer's manual for above-ground pools.
  • In-ground pool plans must be signed and sealed by a West Virginia registered professional and show reinforcement size and spacing, wall thickness, concrete type, depth limits, steps and footings.
  • Bottom-drain and in-ground pools need drain piping details, entrapment-protection device specs, a vacuum-relief method, and flow calculations from a licensed pool installer.
  • The county lists the 2018 International Residential Code and 2018 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code among its adopted codes.
  • Required in-ground pool inspections include excavation, electrical bonding, the in-ground panel before concrete, final electrical and final.

Source: Swimming Pool Requirements and Checklist (Berkeley County Building Permits and Inspections) · Building Codes (Berkeley County)

Morgantown

City of Morgantown Code Enforcement / Building Permits

  • The city's swimming pool permit guidance asks for a site plan showing property lines, setbacks and the pool location, plus construction plans and manufacturer installation instructions.
  • A Morgantown Utility Board storm water permit application should be submitted with the pool permit application.
  • Separate plumbing, mechanical and electrical permits may be needed, and a variance from the Board of Zoning Appeals may be needed if the project does not meet zoning rules.
  • A city pool checklist says pool water may not drain to the storm drain and must connect to the sewer, and asks for a barrier detail and electrical supply detail.

Source: Swimming Pool Permit Guidance (City of Morgantown) · Pools (City of Morgantown checklist) · Building & Construction (City of Morgantown)

Huntington

City of Huntington Inspections and Permits / Planning and ZoningOnline portal: Online building permits (city ePermits)

  • The zoning ordinance defines a household pool as one holding water at least 30 inches deep and serving the residents of one dwelling unit.
  • Household pools may not be in a required front yard, and a pool or elevated deck must meet the accessory-building setback.
  • Enclosure around in-ground household pools must meet the Building Code; above-ground pools need an enclosure at least four feet high.
  • The ordinance requires a drainage method that will not overload a septic system or flood neighboring property.
  • Permits are applied for and paid online, and some permit types need plan review, with status handled by permits@huntingtonwv.gov.

Source: Huntington Zoning Ordinance (revised 9/14/2020) · ePermits Contractor Instructions (City of Huntington, 4/29/25)

West Virginia state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
West Virginia State Building Code (87 CSR 4, State Fire Commission) adopts the IRC (2018 edition effective August 1, 2022, with a 2024-edition update in 2026), enforced only where a city or county adopts it.
Pool barriers
No state barrier statute; barriers follow the ISPSC/IRC provisions where a jurisdiction adopts the State Building Code (the 2026 update adopts the 2024 ISPSC), otherwise local ordinance.
Contractor licensing
West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board (W. Va. Code § 30-42) licenses contractors by classification, including residential and specialty, for residential jobs above $5,000.
Public pools
West Virginia Bureau for Public Health, Legislative Rule 64 CSR 16 (Recreational Water Facilities): construction plans need state environmental engineering approval before work starts.
Engineer licensing
West Virginia State Board of Registration for Professional Engineers

WEST VIRGINIA QUESTIONS

Questions about West Virginia pool plans.

Is the West Virginia State Building Code enforced everywhere?

No. The State Fire Commission's code (87 CSR 4) applies only where a city or county adopts it. Where adopted, local officials enforce the IRC and pool provisions; elsewhere a residential pool may need no building permit.

Who approves public pool plans in West Virginia?

Public and semi-public pools fall under Legislative Rule 64 CSR 16. Construction plans must be approved by the Bureau for Public Health's environmental engineering staff before construction begins, in addition to any local building permit.

Are your West Virginia pool plans sealed by a West Virginia-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in West Virginia reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your West Virginia jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare West Virginia pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in West Virginia?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
