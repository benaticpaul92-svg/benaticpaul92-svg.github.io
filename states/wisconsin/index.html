WISCONSIN · CITY AND COUNTY PERMIT NOTES

Local permit notes for Wisconsin.

Each city and county runs its own pool permit review. These notes come from the pages each office publishes, checked on 15 November 2026. Rules change, so open the linked source or send us the current checklist with your enquiry.

Your city or county is not listed? Name the permit office in the enquiry form. We draw to the checklist it publishes.

Madison (Dane County)

City of Madison Building Inspection / Development Services Center

  • The city's permit chart lists a swimming pool, above or in ground, as needing a building permit from Building Inspection.
  • The city's pool page says a pool must be at least 3 feet from the house or other structures and at least 3 feet from rear or side lot lines, and a side-yard pool needs a larger side setback.
  • In-ground pools must be enclosed by a fence at least 4 feet high with a locking gate; above-ground pools need controlled access such as a removable ladder.
  • A scaled site plan is required, and lakefront lots need a survey by a registered land surveyor; the city advises a zoning review of the site plan before submitting.

Source: Swimming Pools (above or in-ground) (City of Madison Development Services Center) · Permits (City of Madison Development Services Center)

Racine

City of Racine Department of City Development, Division of Building Inspection

  • The city has a dedicated Pool Permit Application for above-ground and in-ground pools.
  • Applications must include the completed form, a site plan of the pool location with dimensions, a construction plan and payment.
  • The application says work done without permits may result in double permit fees.

Source: Pool Permit Application (City of Racine) · Building Inspection (City of Racine)

Menomonee Falls (Waukesha County)

Village of Menomonee Falls Building Inspection / Engineering and DevelopmentOnline portal: Village permit portal (General Building Permit Application)

  • Pool permits can be applied for in person, by mail or through the village permit portal using the General Building Permit Application.
  • The application needs a property survey showing existing structures, the proposed pool, decking, fencing and overhead or underground wiring, plus two copies of pool construction data and depth.
  • An electrical permit is required for all pool installations, and properties on a private sewage system need a Waukesha County Preliminary Site Evaluation first.
  • In-ground pools get an electrical bonding inspection and a final inspection of wiring and fencing (location, height, self-closing and self-latching).

Source: Swimming Pools (Village of Menomonee Falls)

Brookfield (Waukesha County)

City of Brookfield (permit applications page)Online portal: OpenGov online permitting portal

  • The city's permit applications page links a dedicated online Pool Permit.
  • Plumbing, HVAC and other trade permits are listed as separate online applications.

Source: Permit Applications (City of Brookfield)

Wisconsin state-level rules

The state-level rules your drawings are checked against. Cities and counties can add local amendments, so send the jurisdiction with your enquiry.

Residential code
Wisconsin Uniform Dwelling Code (SPS 320 to 325), a state-written code for one- and two-family dwellings administered by DSPS, mandatory statewide; it is not the IRC.
Pool barriers
The UDC does not regulate private pools, so residential pool barriers are set by local (municipal or county) ordinance.
Contractor licensing
No statewide license for residential pool contractors; DSPS Dwelling Contractor and Qualifier certification is required only to obtain UDC dwelling permits (SPS 305.31), and pool permits are local.
Public pools
Wisconsin DSPS, Wis. Admin. Code ch. SPS 390 (public swimming pools and water attractions): plan approval required before construction (SPS 390.04).
Engineer licensing
Wisconsin Examining Board of Architects, Landscape Architects, Professional Engineers, Designers and Professional Land Surveyors (DSPS)

WISCONSIN QUESTIONS

Questions about Wisconsin pool plans.

Does the Wisconsin Uniform Dwelling Code cover residential pools?

No. The UDC (SPS 320 to 325) governs construction of one- and two-family dwellings. Private pools are regulated by municipal and county ordinances, which set the barrier, setback and permit requirements the drawings must meet.

Who reviews public pool plans in Wisconsin?

DSPS reviews and approves plans for public pools, water attractions and slides under SPS 390 before construction or alteration starts. DATCP is copied on plan approvals and handles the operating side once the pool opens.

Are your Wisconsin pool plans sealed by a Wisconsin-licensed engineer?

Yes. A Professional Engineer licensed in Wisconsin reviews the package, takes responsible charge and applies their seal. You receive a submission-ready set for your Wisconsin jurisdiction.

How fast can you prepare Wisconsin pool drawings?

Residential drawing preparation takes 2 business days after we receive all project inputs. PE review and sealing, and the permit office’s own review, have separate timelines.

Do you work to my city or county’s checklist in Wisconsin?

Yes. Send the submittal checklist or the review comments from your permit office. Your quote lists the drawings and calculations that respond to it.
